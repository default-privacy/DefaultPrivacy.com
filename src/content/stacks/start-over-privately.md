A fresh start is mostly about order. Set up the new contact details first, then a mailing address that isn't your home, then move without handing your new address to every mailer in the country. Clean up people-search sites last, once the new records exist, because the sites refill from them.

This is not an overnight job. Like anything worth doing, a fresh start takes time. You have to start somewhere, and Step 1 takes one evening with no move needed.

## The Short Answer

Starting over privately means limiting who gets your new address. It is not a new identity: your bank and federal agencies still get your real details.

1. Leaving someone? See the [survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit). Changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788). Ask about your state's Address Confidentiality Program first.
2. Tonight: a new inbox, aliases and a second number.
3. Before the move: a mailbox address, then masked cards.
4. Moving: tell senders yourself. Skip the permanent change of address.
5. After: keep it off records where you lawfully can, then opt out of people-search sites.

---

## Who this is for, and what it does not do

This is for anyone starting over somewhere new: after a move, a breakup, harassment, or wanting off people-search sites for good. The threat is ordinary and persistent. People-search sites, mailing lists and anyone who looks you up can piece your new address together from the details you hand out in the first few weeks. This plan cuts down how many people and companies get those details.

What it does not do:

- **It does not give you a new identity.** This plan does not change your name, Social Security number or history. Be wary of anyone selling more. The FTC says: "If a company promises to create a new credit identity or hide your bad credit history or bankruptcy, that's also a scam" ([FTC, Fixing your credit FAQs](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fixing-your-credit-faqs)). Even California's official address program says it "does not hide participants or help them to change their identities, social security numbers, or location" ([CA Safe at Home FAQ](https://www.sos.ca.gov/registries/safe-home/frequently-asked-questions)).
- **It does not hide you from the law.** It is not a way to avoid creditors, a court or the police. USPS lists cases where it may share mailbox records, including "for law enforcement purposes" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).
- **It does not erase the past.** Old records stay where they are. People-search sites may compile data from government public records, which the FTC says "may include property records", driving records and voter registration information. And opting out "doesn't delete your information from public records" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)).

## The legal line

Privacy aliases for email, phone numbers and payment cards are fine, and so is a mailbox address for your mail. False names or false addresses on government, bank, lease or credit paperwork are not. Some federal rules that apply:

- The mail-agent form you sign for a mailbox says that anyone who gives false or misleading information on it may face criminal and civil penalties ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).
- Federal law makes it a crime to knowingly and willfully make a materially false statement in a matter within the jurisdiction of the federal executive, legislative or judicial branch ([18 U.S.C. 1001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1001)).
- US banks must get "a residential or business street address" from an individual before opening an account, with fallbacks for people who have none ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)).

So where a bank or federal form asks for your home or residential address, give your real one. For landlords and lenders, check what they require, and never give them false details. Every step below limits who else gets your address.

## Step 0: Safety check first, if you are leaving someone

If you are leaving an abuser or someone who watches you, read this before anything else. The [stalking and abuse survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit) covers the account and device steps in the order that is safest. This page does not repeat them.

**What to do.** Before any step below, know this: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788).

Safety Net, a project of the National Network to End Domestic Violence, warns that "some abusive individuals may escalate their controlling and dangerous behavior if they feel they've lost access to the victim", and suggests using "a different device that the person hasn't had physical or remote access to in the past, and doesn't have access to now" ([Safety Net technology safety plan](https://www.techsafety.org/resources-survivors/technology-safety-plan)).

Two things in this plan are time-sensitive for you:

- **Ask about your state's Address Confidentiality Program (ACP) before you create records at the new address.** Washington's program says the person "should not have created any local or state records using the new address they wish to keep private", and that it "cannot change or delete public records that were created before the person enrolled" ([WA ACP](https://www.sos.wa.gov/statewide-programs/address-confidentiality-program-acp/application-process/acp-appropriate-me)). In California you apply "by meeting, in person, with a victim's assistance counselor at a designated enrolling agency" ([CA Safe at Home FAQ](https://www.sos.ca.gov/registries/safe-home/frequently-asked-questions)). Programs differ by state, so check your own state's page with your advocate.
- **Any USPS change of address sends a letter to the home you are leaving.** USPS says: "After scheduling a Change of Address (COA), USPS will promptly mail, to the address you are leaving, a Move Validation Letter (MVL) to validate the move" ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). If the other person still gets mail there, plan Step 4 with your advocate.

**The limit.** No digital step makes anyone safe. Washington says "ACP participation does not guarantee safety", and that private businesses and federal agencies "are not required to accept the ACP substitute address but may work with a participant upon request" ([WA ACP](https://www.sos.wa.gov/statewide-programs/address-confidentiality-program-acp)).

**How you know it's done.** You have talked with an advocate or the Hotline. You are working from a device the other person never had access to. You know whether your new state has an ACP, and you have applied before creating records at the new address, or decided with your advocate not to.

## Step 1: New inbox, email aliases and a second number (start tonight)

**What to do.** Do these in order, before anything else:

1. A new inbox. Turn on two-step login and save its recovery codes ([leave Gmail without losing your accounts](/guides/leave-gmail-without-losing-your-accounts)).
2. Email aliases that forward to it, one per site. Two common choices are SimpleLogin and Addy: see [email aliases: SimpleLogin vs Addy](/guides/email-aliases-simplelogin-vs-addy).
3. A second phone number. App numbers such as [MySudo](/directory/mysudo) or [JMP](/directory/jmp-chat) are the easiest. Number sellers say a prepaid SIM or eSIM is less likely to be refused for verification codes. See [how to get a second phone number](/guides/get-a-second-phone-number).

**Why it comes first.** Every later step asks for an email and a phone number. The USPS form for a mailbox has fields for both ("4d. Telephone Number", "4e. Email Address") ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). Privacy.com asks for "a US phone number that is able to receive SMS text messages", and doesn't say whether an app number counts ([Privacy.com support](https://support.privacy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012046934-Who-can-use-Privacy)). People-search opt-outs "may require you to give some information to verify your identity" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)). If the new details exist first, you can give those instead of the old ones.

If you are leaving someone, do all of this from the device in Step 0, not one the other person has had access to, and keep the recovery codes somewhere they can't reach. Safety Net's advice is to "not link these new accounts to any old accounts or numbers" ([Safety Net](https://www.techsafety.org/resources-survivors/technology-safety-plan)). So don't verify the new inbox or number with a number the other person knows. A charge for the number, and later for the mailbox, can show up on a card or account they can see. Safety Net says financial abuse of survivors may look like "Monitoring an individual's spending" ([Safety Net, financial abuse](https://www.techsafety.org/financial-abuse-and-technology)), so plan how you pay with your advocate. Changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788).

**The limit.** An alias does not make you anonymous, and neither does a second number. Some services refuse app (VoIP) numbers for verification codes ([second number guide](/guides/get-a-second-phone-number)). Your bank and federal agencies still get your real details.

**How you know it's done.** The new inbox has two-step login and saved recovery codes, and its recovery options don't point at an old or shared number. A test alias reaches the inbox. The second number receives a text.

## Step 2: A mailing address that isn't your home, weeks before the move

**What to do.** While you still live at your old address, rent one of these: a private mailbox (PMB) at a mail-receiving business, a virtual mailbox (a private mailbox that also scans your mail), or a USPS PO Box. Our guide walks through it: [get a mailing address that isn't your home](/guides/get-a-mailing-address-that-isnt-your-home). One example in our directory is [Anytime Mailbox](/directory/anytime-mailbox). Fill in the form with your real details.

**Why now, before the move.** A private mailbox needs USPS PS Form 1583 and two IDs. "The second must confirm the Applicant's or Authorized Individual's address listed on this form", using a license, current lease, insurance policy, mortgage, vehicle registration or voter card ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). While you still live at the old address, your lease, insurance policy, vehicle registration or voter card should already show it. A driver's license can count as the photo ID or the address ID, not both ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). For a PO Box in a new city, USPS says "to begin the verification process, you need to go to a Post Office where you currently live", and "After you move, you will need to complete the verification process at your new address" ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). The mailbox also has to exist before you can point any forwarding at it (Step 4).

**The limit.** The mailbox company and USPS hold your real home address. USPS may share mailbox records in listed cases, including "for law enforcement purposes" and "to agents or contractors when necessary to fulfill a business function". Information about someone "who has filed an appropriate protective court order with the application will not be disclosed except pursuant to the order of a court of competent jurisdiction and subject to the approval of the USPS General Counsel" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). The form asks whether you are a court-ordered protected individual, and if you are, you must attach a copy of the court order (PS Form 1583, item 4k). USPS rules say the address must carry "PMB" (private mailbox) or the "#" sign ([USPS DMM 508](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)). And the form has to be kept current: "when any information required on this form changes or becomes obsolete, the applicant must file an updated application with the agent" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). That update comes in Step 5.

**How you know it's done.** You have the street address and box number, the form is approved, a test letter arrives, and you have a note to update the form after the move.

## Step 3: Masked cards for shopping

**What to do.** Use a masked card for online shopping so stores never see your real card number. In the US, one option is [Privacy.com](/directory/privacy-com). Put the mailbox in as the billing address. Privacy.com says "For orders that require shipping, we recommend using a billing address similar to your shipping address" ([Privacy.com support](https://support.privacy.com/hc/en-us/articles/37945855086487-What-billing-address-should-I-use-with-a-Privacy-Card)), and with the mailbox in place both can be the mailbox from the first order. Outside the US, see [Privacy.com alternatives outside the US](/guides/privacy-com-alternatives-outside-the-us). The [anonymous payments stack](/stacks/anonymous-payments-financial-privacy) covers the wider options.

**Why after the mailbox.** The mailbox company gets your name and ID anyway (Step 2), so masking that one payment buys nothing. After it, every masked card can carry the mailbox address.

**The limit.** A masked card hides your card number, and the shop still sees who you are. Privacy.com says the merchant gets the card number, expiry, CVV and "billing address entered at checkout", and "the merchant will also see your name when you provide it during checkout" ([Privacy.com support](https://support.privacy.com/hc/en-us/articles/24450438423063-What-information-does-Privacy-share-with-the-merchant)). Privacy.com itself asks for "an identification number (for most users, this is an SSN)" ([Privacy.com support](https://support.privacy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012046934-Who-can-use-Privacy)). Use your own name.

**How you know it's done.** A masked card works at one shop with the mailbox as the billing address, and you have removed your regular card from the stores that saved it.

## Step 4: The move. Tell senders yourself and skip the permanent change of address

**What to do.** If you are leaving someone, don't file any change of address, and don't change the address on any account you share with them, such as a joint bank account, an insurance policy or a family phone plan, until you have planned it with your advocate (Step 0). Changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788).

Give your mailbox address yourself to the people and companies that mail you things: shops, subscriptions, doctors, friends. Where a form asks where you live, give your home address. Give your bank, your employer and federal agencies your real details. If you are in your state's ACP, ask the program where you can use its substitute address.

Do not file a permanent USPS change of address. If you want a safety net while senders catch up, file a temporary one and point it at the mailbox, not your new home. USPS says "You may file a COA request to a CMRA" (a commercial mail receiving agency, meaning a private mailbox) ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). That way the forwarding order lists the mailbox, not your new home. Filing online has a $1.25 fee. You enter a card number and billing address, and USPS says it "can electronically verify your card's information with the issuing bank". If you prefer not to use a card, PS Form 3575 from your local Post Office is the alternative ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)).

Tell the IRS yourself. The IRS says a USPS change of address "may update your address of record", but "not all post offices forward government checks, so you should still notify us", and it lists Form 8822 for this ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/irs-procedures/address-changes)). The form's instructions say to enter a P.O. box "instead of your street address only if your post office does not deliver mail to your street address" ([IRS Form 8822](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8822.pdf)).

**Why no permanent change of address.** USPS describes NCOALink as "approximately 160 million permanent change-of-address (COA) records" (as of October 2026), and its licensees "receive 48 months of COA data" or 18 months, depending on the license ([USPS PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/mailing-and-shipping-services/NCOALink)). Mailers use it to update their lists. USPS also says that "During months 13-18, the mail is returned to sender with your new address attached" ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). Our [moving guide](/guides/move-without-landing-on-people-search-sites) has more on how this works.

**The limit.** A temporary change is better, and still not invisible. Some mailers can still learn that you moved, but USPS doesn't give them the new address. USPS's technical guide for licensees says "Temporary COAs are only available in the 48-month product", flagged with "N = New Address not provided" ([NCOALink technical guide](https://postalpro.usps.com/mnt/glusterfs/2023-04/usertechinfo_v12.pdf)). Temporary forwarding has a 15-day minimum and an end date. When it ends, USPS says "delivery will resume to the original address you listed on your COA request", so move every sender to the mailbox before then. Mail from senders that "use ancillary endorsements may not forward", and USPS gives "driver's licenses or bank statements" as examples ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). If you move without filing, USPS holds mail "for up to 10 calendar days. After that, most mail items are returned to sender(s)" (same [USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)), so tell senders before you go. And any change of address sends a letter to your old home (Step 0).

**How you know it's done.** Your key senders show the mailbox address in their own records. No permanent change of address is on file. Any temporary one points at the mailbox, and you have kept its confirmation code.

## Step 5: Records at the new address

This is the step where your new address enters official records. Do it carefully, once. Some records have legal deadlines for a new address, so check each one. If you are waiting on an ACP application, ask your advocate how the deadlines fit.

**What to do.**

- **Lease and utilities.** Ask what they require and whether bills and notices can go to your mailbox. Never give them false details. If your credit is frozen, the FTC says you can temporarily lift the freeze to rent an apartment and put it back when you're done ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/credit-freezes-and-fraud-alerts); see [how to freeze your credit](/guides/how-to-freeze-your-credit)).
- **Voter registration.** On the federal form, your home goes in Box 2 ("Do not put your mailing address here if it is different from your home address"), and Box 3 is for a different mailing address ([EAC National Mail Voter Registration Form](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/eac_assets/1/6/Federal_Voter_Registration_ENG.pdf)). Ask your state election office what its voter file shows and whether you can mark your record confidential.
- **Driver's license.** For a REAL ID, federal rules print your principal residence, but "an alternative address may be displayed" where state law, regulation or DMV procedure allows it, and for people enrolled in a state address confidentiality program ([6 CFR 37.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/6/37.17)). Ask your DMV. Other licenses vary by state.
- **Your mailbox form.** File the updated PS Form 1583 with your new home address. A current lease is one of the accepted address IDs ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).
- **Your bank.** Update it with your new home address. If you share the account with the person you are leaving, plan this with your advocate first (Step 4).

**The limit.** Government records stay public where the law makes them public. The FTC says opting out of people search sites "doesn't delete your information from public records, so someone could find public records about you on a government website" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)). An ACP can't remove records made before you enrolled (Step 0).

**How you know it's done.** Your lease, utilities, voter record and license show the mailbox, ACP or confidential address where that is allowed, and your real address where it is required. Your bank and your PS Form 1583 have your new home address. You have looked yourself up on your state and county record searches.

## Step 6: People-search cleanup, after the move, then on repeat

**What to do.** Once the new records exist, search your name on the major people-search sites and file each opt-out. Use an alias email for opt-outs and give only what each form requires. Don't type your new number or city into a broker's form unless the listing already shows it. Our [broker removal guide](/guides/remove-name-from-data-brokers) has the steps, and the [delete yourself from the internet stack](/stacks/delete-yourself-from-the-internet) covers paid services and the free lists. If you now live in California, the state's free Delete Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP) lets you "send one request to over 600 registered data brokers". From August 1, 2026, brokers "must access DROP at least once every 45 days to begin processing deletion requests", and it "could take up to 90 days to see your status updated in DROP" ([CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop/)). It is for California residents only.

**Why last.** The FTC says "if the information in your public records changes, your information could re-appear for sale on people search sites", and advises: "Periodically check the people search sites. If you find any new information about yourself, submit a new opt-out request" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)). Clean up before Step 5 and you do the work twice.

**The limit.** Public records stay public. The FTC also warns that "your information may still appear in the reports of your relatives, neighbors, or associates" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)). This is a recurring job, and no primary source we found says how many weeks it takes for a new address to show up.

**How you know it's done.** A search for your name shows no current listing on the major sites, and you have a reminder to check again. Our broker guide suggests every 90 days.

## Step 7 (optional): Buying a home? Ask a lawyer before you sign

**What to do.** If you buy, property records are among the public records the FTC says people-search sites may use ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)). Before you sign a purchase contract, ask a local real estate attorney how and when your deed is recorded, what your state lets you shield, and how a trust or LLC would affect your mortgage and insurance. Mortgage rules matter here: Fannie Mae says it buys mortgages "made to borrowers who are natural persons", with listed exceptions for some trusts ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide B2-2-01](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-2-01/general-borrower-eligibility-requirements)). Our [moving guide](/guides/move-without-landing-on-people-search-sites) covers this too. If you rent, skip this step.

**Why it is timed before the purchase.** The attorney's answer can change how you buy, so get it while the deal can still change.

**The limit.** An anonymous LLC is business privacy. It does not hide you from the IRS, your bank or a court ([what an anonymous LLC does not do](/guides/what-anonymous-llc-does-not-do)). Don't put a false name on a deed.

**How you know it's done.** You have the attorney's answer in writing before the contract, and after closing the county property search shows what you expected.

## Tradeoffs at a glance

| Step | Effort | What it keeps private | What it can't do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Safety check and ACP | An advocate call, an in-person ACP appointment in some states | Your new address on state and local records, in states with an ACP | Guarantee safety, or erase records made before you enrolled |
| 1. Inbox, aliases, second number | One evening, no move needed | Your real email and number from shops, sign-ups and later forms | Make you anonymous, or pass every verification check |
| 2. Mailing address | A form, two IDs and a rental fee | Your home address from anyone you mail with | Hide your address from the mailbox company, USPS or the law |
| 3. Masked cards | Minutes per store | Your real card number | Hide your name or billing address from the shop |
| 4. The move | Telling each sender yourself | Your new home from NCOALink mailers | Stop mail that won't forward, the letter to your old home, or delivery there when forwarding ends |
| 5. Records | A few forms after the move | The address on records that allow a mailbox, ACP or confidential flag | Keep required public records private |
| 6. Cleanup | A few hours, repeated | Current listings on people-search sites | Remove public-record data or relatives' listings |
| 7. Property (optional) | An attorney's fee | Depends on your state, so ask the attorney | Hide you from your bank, the IRS or a court |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is this a new identity?

No. This plan does not change your name or Social Security number. Your bank and federal agencies still get your real details ([18 U.S.C. 1001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1001); [31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)). The FTC calls promises of "a new credit identity" a scam ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fixing-your-credit-faqs)). If you are in danger, an advocate can explain the official options in your state. Start with Step 0 and the [survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit).

### Can I start before I move?

Yes, and you should. Step 1 needs no move at all, and the mailbox in Step 2 is easier while your lease or other address ID still shows your current home ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).

### Should I file a USPS change of address?

Not a permanent one. USPS licenses permanent change-of-address records to companies that update mailing lists, and some licensees get up to 48 months of them ([USPS PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/mailing-and-shipping-services/NCOALink)). If you need forwarding for a few weeks, file a temporary one pointed at your mailbox. Any change of address also sends a letter to the address you are leaving ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)).

### Can I give my bank the mailbox address?

Give your bank your real home address as your residential address. Banks must get "a residential or business street address" from an individual before opening an account ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)).

### What if I am leaving someone?

Start with Step 0 and the [survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit), and know this: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788). Ask about your state's ACP before you create records at the new address ([WA ACP](https://www.sos.wa.gov/statewide-programs/address-confidentiality-program-acp/application-process/acp-appropriate-me)).

### How long until I'm off people-search sites?

We found no primary source that gives a timeline. The FTC says to "Periodically check the people search sites" and file again when new information shows up ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)). Plan on it as a habit.
