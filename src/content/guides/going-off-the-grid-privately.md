"Off the grid" means two things. Online, it means fewer accounts, fewer connected devices and less data tied to your name. Physically, it means a home that isn't hooked up to the power grid, often with solar power, a well and a septic system.

Both cut what companies collect about you. Neither takes you off government records.

## The Short Answer

Online, going off the grid cuts what companies collect. Living off the grid physically drops the power company, but not the public records. Neither takes you off government records.

1. Close old accounts, then use email aliases and a second number for new ones.
2. Pay cash for small things and use masked cards online.
3. Switch off or drop smart devices you don't need.
4. If you buy land, expect a recorded deed, a tax roll entry and permits on file.
5. Use your real name and address on government, bank, lease and credit paperwork.
6. If you are getting away from someone, read the safety section below before you change anything.

## If you are trying to get away from someone

If you are going off the grid to get away from a person, know this first: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788). Do the steps below from a device and account the person has never had access to.

## Living off the grid physically: the records that still follow you

Making your own power drops the power bill. It doesn't drop the paperwork. Rules vary by state and county. The counties below are examples only.

**Building permits.** For example, Apache County, Arizona, answers "My property is off grid. Do I still need a building permit?" with "Yes. Any living quarters, solar electric systems, and outbuildings over 200 square feet require a building permit" ([Apache County Building Safety FAQ, archived March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250305050639/https://www.apachecountyaz.gov/accnt_591117/site_591118/Documents/FAQ-Building-Safety.pdf)). The county site blocked our check, so this is an archived copy and may be out of date.

**A street address and a tax bill.** In Madison County, North Carolina, "An address must be assigned before the permits office can officially issue a building permit for a new structure." The same county's assessor "lists and values all real and personal property subject to ad valorem (according to value) taxation on an assessment roll each year" ([Madison County Tax Assessor](https://www.madisoncountync.gov/tax-assessor.html)).

**Septic systems.** "EPA does not regulate single family home septic systems. In most states, local health departments issue construction and operating permits to install septic systems under state laws that govern public health protection and abatement of public nuisances." EPA adds that your deed, building permit and design plans "will likely contain information about the presence (or lack) of a septic system" ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/septic/frequent-questions-septic-systems)).

**Private wells.** EPA says "The quality and safety of drinking water from private domestic wells are not regulated by the Federal Government under the Safe Drinking Water Act nor by most state governments and laws" ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/privatewells)). That is about water quality. Drilling a well can still need a permit, and a permit can put your name in a database. For example, Delaware "manages and issues well construction and use permits", and says its "Well permit data in the Portal can be searched by property owner name, permit number and geographic location" ([Delaware DNREC](https://dnrec.delaware.gov/water/residential/well-permits/)).

**The deed.** If you buy the land, the deed is recorded. For example, Solano County, California, keeps deeds "in perpetuity," and says "These documents are considered Official Public Records and are generally available for public access" ([Solano County Recorder](https://www.solanocounty.gov/government/assessorrecorder/recorder-division/obtaining-record-copies-deeds-liens-etc)). Public doesn't always mean online. Sacramento County's assessor says "Due to privacy concerns owner information is not available online," but it "is available by contacting the Assessor's Office" ([Sacramento County Assessor](https://assessor.saccounty.gov/content/assessor/us/en/maps-property-data-and-records/assessor-records.html)). Before you buy, ask a local real estate attorney what your state lets you shield. [Keeping your home address off public records](/guides/keep-home-address-off-public-records) covers what you can do yourself.

## Records that stay either way

- **Your Social Security number stays.** "You need a Social Security number (SSN) to work," and "Many financial institutions, such as banks and credit companies, also ask for your number when you open an account" ([SSA, archived October 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20261015220403/https://www.ssa.gov/pubs/EN-05-10002.pdf)). The SSA site blocked our direct check.
- **Taxes stay.** Generally, you need to file if your income is over the filing requirement for your filing status and age, if you have "over $400 in net earnings from self-employment (side jobs or other independent work)", or if other situations apply ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/check-if-you-need-to-file-a-tax-return)). Land is taxed too, as the county examples above show.
- **Voter records are often public.** You can usually register to vote without a street address. The federal form says "If you live in a rural area but do not have a street address, or if you have no address, please show where you live using the map in Box C" ([EAC, National Mail Voter Registration Form](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/eac_assets/1/6/Federal_Voter_Registration_ENG.pdf)). Voter lists "are often subject to public records laws, which require their disclosure to some individuals" ([EAC, Voter Roll Privacy](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Voter_Roll_Privacy.pdf)).
- **A car still means a DMV record.** Federal law limits who a state DMV can give it to, with exceptions such as "For use by any government agency, including any court or law enforcement agency" ([18 U.S.C. 2721](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2721)).

## The legal line

Privacy aliases for email, phone and cards are fine, and so is a mailbox for your mail. False names or false addresses on government, bank, lease or credit paperwork are not.

- Federal law makes it a crime to knowingly and willfully make a materially false statement in a matter within the jurisdiction of the federal executive, legislative or judicial branch ([18 U.S.C. 1001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1001)).
- US banks must get a residential or business street address from an individual before opening an account ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)). If you have no street address, the rule allows "an Army Post Office (APO) or Fleet Post Office (FPO) box number, or the residential or business street address of next of kin or of another contact individual". It doesn't list a PO Box or a private mailbox.

This guide doesn't help anyone hide from creditors, courts or police.

Ask your county building and health offices before you build, drill a well or put in a septic system.

## How you know you're done

Online: your old accounts are closed, new sign-ups get an alias and a second number, your devices are trimmed and your mail goes to a mailbox.

Physically: you have checked your county's permit, septic and well rules before building, and you know which records carry your name: the deed, the tax roll, permits, your voter record and your DMV record.

## FAQ

**Is it legal to live off the grid?**

It depends on where you live. We found no single national rule. For example, Apache County, Arizona, says an off-grid property still needs a building permit for any living quarters and solar electric system ([Apache County, archived March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250305050639/https://www.apachecountyaz.gov/accnt_591117/site_591118/Documents/FAQ-Building-Safety.pdf)). Ask your county building and health offices.

**Does living off the grid take me off public records?**

No. If you own the land, the deed is recorded and the land is on the tax roll. For example, Solano County, California, says deeds "are considered Official Public Records and are generally available for public access" ([Solano County Recorder](https://www.solanocounty.gov/government/assessorrecorder/recorder-division/obtaining-record-copies-deeds-liens-etc)).

**Do I still have to file taxes if I live off the grid?**

Where your power comes from doesn't change it. Generally, you need to file if your income is over the filing requirement for your filing status and age, if you have "over $400 in net earnings from self-employment (side jobs or other independent work)", or if other situations apply ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/check-if-you-need-to-file-a-tax-return)).

**Can I register to vote without a street address?**

The federal form allows for it. If you have no street address, it asks you to show where you live on the map in Box C ([EAC](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/eac_assets/1/6/Federal_Voter_Registration_ENG.pdf)). Check your state's instructions on the form. The form says Wyoming "cannot accept this form", North Dakota "does not have voter registration", and New Hampshire and Wisconsin accept it "only as a request" for their own form.
