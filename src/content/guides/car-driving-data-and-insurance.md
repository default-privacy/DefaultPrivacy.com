**Last checked: October 2026** · Claims below are what each company or agency says, with a link (start with [the FTC's order against GM](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423052c4828gmlexisfinalorder.pdf)); app and car menus change often; lawsuits are allegations unless a ruling or order is named; this is not legal advice.

Your car may be reporting how you drive. This is called telematics: data a car or phone sends about where it goes, how fast, and how hard it brakes. For some drivers, that data ended up with insurers.

The clearest case on record is GM. The FTC alleged that many drivers "discovered these consumer reports only after receiving adverse action notices from insurance companies," and that "their car insurance policies were being denied or cancelled, or their premiums increased" ([FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423052c4828gmlexisfinalcomplaint.pdf)).

One question runs through this guide:

**Who has my driving record, and how do I stop adding to it?**

---

## Step 1: See what they already have

Companies that sell driving reports to insurers are consumer reporting agencies: firms that build reports about people for decisions like insurance or credit. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) lets you see those reports and dispute errors.

### Arity Services (Allstate's data company)

Arity is a driving-data company owned by Allstate. Allstate calls it "an Allstate affiliate" ([Allstate](https://www.allstate.com/drivewise)). Its consumer reporting arm, Arity Services, says insurers may consider "your driving data collected by insurers and/or mobile applications." It says companies send it this data when you agree to use your driving data for an insurance quote ([Arity](https://arity.com/arity-services-data-support/)).

- **Get the report.** Go to arityservices.consumerprivacyinfo.com and choose "Request Consumer Report." You get one free copy every 12 months. Questions go to arityservices@arity.com ([Arity](https://arity.com/arity-services-data-support/)).
- **Dispute it.** Use the same portal and choose "Dispute Consumer Report." Arity says results come "in writing within thirty (30) days" ([Arity](https://arity.com/arity-services-data-support/)).

For access or deletion, use arity.consumerprivacyinfo.com or call 1-800-654-1412 ([Arity](https://arity.com/privacy/)).

### LexisNexis Risk Solutions

- **Get the report.** Use the online form at [consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request) and pick "Request Your Consumer Disclosure Report." You need your name, date of birth, address, and either your Social Security number or your driver's license number and state ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request)). You can also request it by phone at 1-866-897-8126, or by mail with the printable form ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/consumer)). For questions, LexisNexis lists its Consumer Center at 1-888-497-0011 ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request)). The online form says email is optional, but you must add and verify one to include "email-linked data" in your report ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request)).
- **What to expect.** A letter comes by mail first, explaining how to see the report online ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/consumer)).

No LexisNexis consumer page we read says the report includes telematics. It gives examples such as property records, liens, judgments, bankruptcies, professional licenses and past addresses ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/consumer)).

But Kia's own policy, updated Dec 16, 2025, says that when Kia Connect is active, "certain driving behavior data may be shared with our third-party data provider, currently LexisNexis Risk Solutions, to help generate your Driving Score." The listed fields include trip time stamps, seatbelt status, speed and "precise geolocation information (latitude/longitude coordinates)" ([Kia](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/privacy-policy.html)).

In 2024, the New York Times reported on a Chevrolet Bolt driver whose LexisNexis report listed 640 trips, with times, distance, speeding and hard braking ([New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20240311090514/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/03/11/technology/carmakers-driver-tracking-insurance.html) (archived copy, March 11, 2024)).

A state privacy opt-out or delete request may not remove insurance report data. LexisNexis warns that opt-outs do not apply "to information that is exempt from the requirements of State Privacy Laws" ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request)). FCRA data is generally exempt from those laws. LexisNexis does not say this about driving data. It is our reading.

### Verisk (only for GM, Honda and Hyundai owners)

Verisk stopped its driving report product in 2024 and said it "no longer receives this data from these automakers" ([The Record](https://therecord.media/data-broker-shuts-product-driver-patterns)). Our reading: anything it still holds would be from before mid-2024.

- **What you need.** Your name as owner, addresses from the past 5 years, your signature, and the VIN. The VIN is the 17-character vehicle identification number on your registration ([Verisk form](https://fcra.verisk.com/app/assets/pdfs/driving-behavior-form.pdf)).
- **How to send it.** The form lists Verisk's Consumer Inquiry Center at P.O. Box 5404, Mt. Laurel, NJ 08054-5404, fax 800-955-2422, email FCRA-NPC@verisk.com and phone 800-709-8842. It does not say which of these to use for sending the form. On the form you can choose to get your report back by mail or fax ([Verisk form](https://fcra.verisk.com/app/assets/pdfs/driving-behavior-form.pdf)).
- **Online.** Verisk's FCRA site has a "Driving Data" tab for the same request ([Verisk](https://fcra.verisk.com/)).

**Tip:** Pull the LexisNexis and Arity reports again in 3 to 6 months, after you finish Step 2. Compare them.

## Step 2: Stop the sources

Switching off connected services reduces what your car collects and shares. It may not stop everything. Several carmakers say some data is still collected after you switch off, for example [Nissan](https://www.nissanusa.com/connect/privacy), [Honda](https://web.archive.org/web/20261002020933/https://www.honda.com/privacy/connected-product-privacy-notice) (archived copy, Oct 2, 2026), [GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy) and [Stellantis](https://www.jeep.com/crossbrand_us/privacy). Deleting old data is a separate request. Do both.

### GM (Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, Cadillac)

- **In the car.** GM says "some model year 2019 and later vehicles may have an option to disable location services for OnStar" in the "Privacy Settings in your vehicle's infotainment system." GM does not give the exact submenu. It may still collect location in an emergency, such as a crash ([GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy)).
- **Online.** GM's privacy statement names the "Data and Privacy" section of your GM account (experience.gm.com/myaccount/privacy) only for exterior camera and sensor data consent. It does not say this section stops OnStar location or driving data, so also use one of the other steps here ([GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy)).
- **Full disconnect.** Call 1-888-466-7827 (1-888-4ONSTAR) or press the blue OnStar button to "disconnect your vehicle and unenroll from OnStar." GM says it may still collect location and vehicle data when the blue button is pressed, and for electric cars, when a high voltage battery overheats ([GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy)). The FTC order says GM must let people who unenroll from OnStar switch off its remote data collection, with listed exceptions such as safety and software updates ([FTC order](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423052c4828gmlexisfinalorder.pdf)). When you call, ask that remote data collection be turned off as well.
- **Copy and delete.** Use the U.S. Consumer Privacy Request Form at [gm.com/consumer-privacy](https://www.gm.com/consumer-privacy) or call 1-866-697-7482. GM does not take requests by email. It allows up to 45 days ([GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy)).

### Toyota

Toyota gives an exact path. In the Toyota app, "select the person icon in the top right, select 'Account', select 'Data Privacy Portal', and then select the applicable vehicle" ([Toyota](https://www.toyota.com/privacyvts/)).

There you can decline the Vehicle Connectivity Consent. Toyota says declining "will turn off all data transmission on your vehicle, and you will no longer have access to Connected Services" ([Toyota](https://www.toyota.com/privacyvts/)).

Toyota adds: "If you do nothing, Toyota will continue to use your vehicle's non-precise location, driving and vehicle health data for internal research and data analysis" ([Toyota](https://www.toyota.com/privacyvts/)).

For deletion, use the [Toyota Privacy Hub](https://privacy.toyota.com/) or call 855-226-4048 ([Toyota](https://www.toyota.com/privacyvts/)).

### Nissan

Nissan lists three ways to decline NissanConnect: decline or limit services in the car or app, log in to the Nissan Owners Portal and click "Delete Vehicle & Unsubscribe," or call 1-844-711-8100. It adds that "some limited vehicle data may be collected" regardless ([Nissan](https://www.nissanusa.com/connect/privacy)).

Requests go through Nissan's Privacy Rights Request Form or 1-877-647-2272. After a deletion request, "you will need to confirm that you want your information deleted" ([Nissan](https://www.nissanusa.com/connect/privacy)).

### Stellantis (Jeep, Ram, Dodge, Chrysler, Wagoneer, Alfa Romeo, FIAT)

Letting your subscription lapse does not automatically stop all data collection from your car. Stellantis says: "Expiration or termination of your Connected Services subscription does not automatically stop all collection of personal data from your vehicle" ([Stellantis](https://www.jeep.com/crossbrand_us/privacy)).

To stop it, call and ask to "CANCEL FOR PRIVACY REASONS" ([Stellantis](https://www.jeep.com/crossbrand_us/privacy)):

- SiriusXM Guardian subscribers: 844-796-4827.
- Vehicle Connect subscribers (Jeep Connect, Ram Connect, Dodge Connect and others): 800-777-3600.

Deletion requests go through its online portal or 1-866-221-6871 ([Stellantis](https://www.jeep.com/crossbrand_us/privacy)).

### Tesla

We could only read archived copies of Tesla's pages from 2025.

- **In the car.** Tesla says you may turn certain collection on or off at "Software > Data Sharing" on the touchscreen ([Tesla](https://web.archive.org/web/20250427205648/https://www.tesla.com/legal/privacy) (archived copy, April 27, 2025)).
- **Full stop.** You have to "contact us to deactivate connectivity." Tesla warns this "may result in your vehicle suffering from reduced functionality, serious damage, or inoperability" ([Tesla](https://web.archive.org/web/20250427205648/https://www.tesla.com/legal/privacy) (archived copy, April 27, 2025)).
- **Copy and delete.** Sign in at tesla.com/contactus and choose "Data Privacy Request." Downloading does not delete. Deletion is under "Delete My Tesla Account." Tesla says all active Tesla products must be removed from your account first, and you will lose access to them ([Tesla](https://web.archive.org/web/20250307182356/https://www.tesla.com/support/privacy) (archived copy, March 7, 2025)).

### Kia

Kia's policy says that when Kia Connect is active, Driving Score data may be shared with LexisNexis (see Step 1). The data LexisNexis receives includes your "date of consent" ([Kia](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/privacy-policy.html)), so check whether you agreed to Driving Score. Switching it off is awkward. Kia says to "contact your Vehicle technician who can determine whether ... Kia Connect Services Hardware can be deactivated or uninstalled" ([Kia](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/privacy-policy.html)).

If you also join Kia's optional insurance program, Kia says LexisNexis may share your score with insurers ([Kia](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/privacy-policy.html)). Requests go to Kia's portal at customercare.kiausa.com or (800) 333-4542 ([Kia](https://www.kia.com/us/en/privacy)).

### Ford and Lincoln

To stop a car's Connectivity Device from sharing data, Ford says to use the Connectivity Settings in the Ford or Lincoln app, or remove the vehicle from your Ford or Lincoln account. A master (factory) reset disconnects a modem-equipped car from any Ford or Lincoln accounts. If your car has a Connectivity Device, you must also remove the VIN from your account to stop account-related sharing. If a master reset isn't available, Ford says to do a Connectivity Reset ([Ford](https://web.archive.org/web/20261006015226/https://www.ford.com/help/privacy/) (archived copy, Oct 6, 2026)).

Ford warns: "Deleting the Ford/Lincoln app from your device without also performing a MASTER/FACTORY RESET will not disable data sharing from the vehicle." Requests: 1-800-392-3673 or the form linked from [Ford's privacy page](https://web.archive.org/web/20261006015226/https://www.ford.com/help/privacy/) (archived copy, Oct 6, 2026).

### Brands with no verified menu path

For these brands, we could not find the exact steps on any official page. Use the request route below and check your owner's manual.

- **Honda and Acura.** Honda says you can opt out "through in-vehicle or smart device-based settings" and to "consult your owner's manual." It still collects "limited data" for software updates and safety features ([Honda](https://web.archive.org/web/20261002020933/https://www.honda.com/privacy/connected-product-privacy-notice) (archived copy, Oct 2, 2026)). Requests: Honda's [Your Privacy Choices page](https://web.archive.org/web/20261007105043/https://www.honda.com/privacy/your-privacy-choices) (archived copy, Oct 7, 2026).
- **Hyundai.** Hyundai says that to withdraw consent for precise location, you "unenroll from your Bluelink account." It gives no steps for other data. Its separate Vehicle Technologies and Services Privacy Notice may have more. Requests: the [Personal Information Request Portal](https://owners.hyundaiusa.com/us/en/privacy/data-request.html) or (800) 633-5151 ([Hyundai](https://www.hyundaiusa.com/us/en/privacy-policy)).
- **Lexus.** Toyota's notice covers the Toyota app and does not mention Lexus, so we did not list Toyota's steps for Lexus ([Toyota](https://www.toyota.com/privacyvts/)). Check your owner's manual.
- **Subaru.** Subaru offers opt-out and deletion webforms to all U.S. residents on its [consumer privacy page](https://www.subaru.com/support/consumer-privacy.html). We found no official page on STARLINK settings.
- **BMW.** BMW says to use "the CarData section within your My BMW Garage Vehicle Dashboard," or call (855) 811-2309 ([BMW](https://web.archive.org/web/20260930184339/https://login.bmwusa.com/pm2/pm-document-service/api/v1link/documents/policyName/MY_BMW_APP_DPP/US/PDF?language=en&KeyId=dbf0a542-ebd1-4ff0-a9a7-55172fbfce35) (archived copy, Sept 30, 2026)).

### Phone apps

Your phone can report your driving too. Arity puts its code, called an SDK, inside other companies' apps. Arity says it collects "device speed, data from your device's accelerometer, gyroscope" and "precise geolocation information" this way. It uses them to work out braking, acceleration and phone handling ([Arity](https://arity.com/privacy/)).

A class action alleges Arity's code was in apps including Life360, GasBuddy and Fuel Rewards. In March 2026 a court let parts of it go forward ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/03/19/court-allows-suit-alleging-allstate-collected-and-sold-consumer-data-to-move-forward-dismisses-portion/)). These are allegations.

Arity's privacy statement gives no way to turn off its code inside another company's app. It does offer an opt-out webform for using your driving and location data in advertising ([Arity](https://arity.com/privacy/)). That limits ad use. It does not stop collection. So the main steps happen on your phone:

- Set location for these apps to "Never" or "While Using," and turn off motion and fitness access.
- **Life360:** in the app's settings, "Privacy & Security" has "Your Privacy Choices." Life360 also lists "Limit Drive Detection" among its app settings ([Life360](https://web.archive.org/web/20260302092021/https://life360-legal.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy) (archived copy, March 2, 2026)). Our [family location apps guide](/guides/family-location-apps-and-airtags) has the full steps.
- **GasBuddy:** in the app, go to "Privacy Rights," then "Location Privacy" ([GasBuddy](https://www.gasbuddy.com/privacy)).

### Insurance programs you joined

Some insurers trade a discount for your driving data. Read the rate wording.

- **Progressive Snapshot:** "Your rate may increase with high-risk driving. But, only about 2 out of 10 drivers actually get an increase" ([Progressive](https://www.progressive.com/auto/discounts/snapshot/)). To leave, log in or call 1-877-329-7283 (in some states you may have to call). You lose any participation discount. Progressive says "In some states and situations, if you opt out more than 45 days after enrollment, you may receive a surcharge to your policy at the next renewal," and data already shared "may impact your renewal rate" ([Progressive FAQ](https://www.progressive.com/auto/discounts/snapshot/snapshot-faq/)).
- **Allstate Drivewise:** the data comes from Arity. Allstate says "drivers frequently exhibiting riskier behaviors could see their rates increased (except NY)" ([Allstate](https://www.allstate.com/drivewise)). We found no page explaining how to leave.
- **State Farm Drive Safe & Save:** State Farm says your premium adjustment "may increase or decrease at each renewal," and your premium may rise if you drive more miles than a low-mileage estimate ([State Farm](https://www.statefarm.com/insurance/auto/discounts/drive-safe-save)). We found no page explaining how to leave.

## Step 3: Make sure it worked

Opt-outs can fail without telling you.

**Ford.** California's privacy agency, CalPrivacy, fined Ford $375,703 in March 2026 ([CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/03/ford-to-change-practices-pay-fine-for-adding-unnecessary-friction-to-opt-out-process/)). Ford had emailed people who opted out and asked them to click a confirmation button. When people didn't click, their requests were deemed "expired." That "resulted in Ford not processing dozens of requests to opt-out within the period required by the CCPA," and "Ford continued to sell/share these consumers' personal information" ([CalPrivacy order](https://privacy.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/357/2026/03/Order-of-Decision-Ford-Motor-Co.pdf)). Ford has since processed them ([CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/03/ford-to-change-practices-pay-fine-for-adding-unnecessary-friction-to-opt-out-process/)). Ford settled without admitting or denying the findings ([CalPrivacy order](https://privacy.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/357/2026/03/Order-of-Decision-Ford-Motor-Co.pdf)).

**Hyundai.** From July 2025 to June 2026, Hyundai and Genesis together received 3,666 California opt-out requests. They denied 384 because they "could not locate the consumer" ([Hyundai](https://www.hyundaiusa.com/us/en/privacy-policy)).

What to do:

1. **Watch your inbox, including spam.** Click any confirmation link. Nissan requires you to confirm a deletion ([Nissan](https://www.nissanusa.com/connect/privacy)).
2. **Use the details the brand has on file.** Same name, email and VIN.
3. **Keep records.** Save the confirmation number or a dated screenshot.
4. **Note the deadline.** GM allows up to 45 days ([GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy)). Kia says opt-outs take up to 15 business days ([Kia](https://www.kia.com/us/en/privacy)).
5. **Re-check.** Open the app setting again later. Then re-pull your Arity and LexisNexis reports.

## What happened

### The FTC order against GM

The FTC alleged that GM collected location "every three seconds, starting from ignition on," tied it to the VIN, and sold driving data to LexisNexis and Verisk ([FTC complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423052c4828gmlexisfinalcomplaint.pdf)).

The final order, Docket No. C-4828, was issued Jan 13, 2026 and announced the next day ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/01/ftc-finalizes-order-settling-allegations-gm-onstar-collected-sold-geolocation-data-without-consumers)). It settles the FTC's allegations. No court ruled on them. Under the order ([FTC order](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423052c4828gmlexisfinalorder.pdf)):

- For five years, GM "must not disclose Covered Driver Data" to anyone acting as a consumer reporting agency.
- GM must get "Affirmative Express Consent" before collecting, using or sharing that data, with exceptions such as emergencies and recalls.
- Within 180 days, GM must delete old driving data unless the owner consents or the data is deidentified (with other listed exceptions), and ask the companies it shared with to delete it.
- All U.S. consumers must have "a simple, easily-located means" to get a copy and ask for deletion.
- GM "must allow consumers to disable the collection of Location Data" where the car supports it.

The FTC says the order lasts 20 years ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/01/ftc-finalizes-order-settling-allegations-gm-onstar-collected-sold-geolocation-data-without-consumers)).

### California's $12.75 million GM settlement

California Attorney General Rob Bonta and the district attorneys of San Francisco, Los Angeles, Napa and Sonoma counties announced a $12.75 million settlement with GM in May 2026, with support from CalPrivacy. The complaint alleges GM sold Californians' driving and location data to Verisk and LexisNexis from 2020 to 2024. The settlement "is subject to court approval" ([CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/05/when-it-comes-to-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-in-the-drivers-seat-attorney-general-bonta-partners-secure-12-75-million-general-motors-privacy-settlement/)). We did not find a court approval as of this writing.

### Honda's $632,500 fine

In March 2025, California's privacy agency (then called the CPPA, now CalPrivacy) fined Honda $632,500. The settlement order found that Honda's request webform required "at least eight data fields," even for a simple opt-out. Honda neither admitted nor denied that finding ([CalPrivacy order](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/20250307_hmc_order.pdf), [CalPrivacy](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250312.html)).

### State lawsuits

All of these are allegations.

- **Arkansas** sued GM and OnStar on Feb 26, 2025 ([Arkansas AG](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/attorney-general-griffin-sues-general-motors-and-onstar-for-deceiving-arkansans-and-unlawfully-selling-data/)).
- **Nebraska** sued them on July 8, 2025, alleging dealers enrolled some customers "without any consent at all" ([Nebraska AG](https://ago.nebraska.gov/attorney-general-mike-hilgers-files-lawsuit-against-general-motors-deceptive-collection-and-sale)).
- **Iowa** sued them on Feb 26, 2026 ([Iowa AG](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/newsroom/attorney-general-brenna-bird-sues-general-motors-for-illegal-business-practices/)).
- **Texas** sued Allstate and Arity on Jan 13, 2025. It alleges they collected driving data from people's phones through software hidden in apps such as Life360, without notice or consent ([Texas AG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-sues-allstate-and-arity-unlawfully-collecting-using-and-selling-over-45)).
- **Montana** opened an investigation of Ford and Stellantis on April 10, 2026. It is not a lawsuit ([Montana DOJ](https://dojmt.gov/attorney-general-knudsen-launches-investigation-into-major-auto-companies/)).

### The federal class action

Private suits against GM, OnStar, LexisNexis and Verisk were combined into one case, In re: Consumer Vehicle Driving Data Tracking Litigation, MDL 3115, in federal court in Georgia. According to the plaintiffs' lawyers, on April 22, 2026 the judge "largely denied defendants' efforts to dismiss federal wiretap and privacy claims" ([DiCello Levitt](https://dicellolevitt.com/court-allows-gm-onstar-vehicle-data-privacy-lawsuit-to-move-forward/)). We found no class certification, settlement or trial.

### What the data sold for

Senators Ron Wyden and Ed Markey wrote to the FTC in July 2024. They said Verisk paid Honda $25,920 for data from 97,000 cars, "or 26 cents per car." Verisk paid Hyundai $1,043,315.69 for 1.7 million vehicles, "or 61 cents per car" ([Wyden-Markey letter](https://www.wyden.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/wyden-markey_auto_privacy_letter_to_ftc.pdf)).

The same letter says Verisk's contracts "did not require that driver telematics data only be used to provide discounts" ([Wyden-Markey letter](https://www.wyden.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/wyden-markey_auto_privacy_letter_to_ftc.pdf)).

## The 2026 study: car apps and ad tech

Northeastern University researchers tested 21 vehicles from 19 brands using Consumer Reports' cars. Consumer Reports reported the results on Sept 29, 2026 ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/personal-information/your-car-is-sharing-data-with-big-tech-companies-study-finds-a4474820962/), [Northeastern paper](https://automatictransmission.khoury.northeastern.edu/paper.pdf)).

The paper says "all but two of the vehicles tested send traffic to at least one third party" ([Northeastern paper](https://automatictransmission.khoury.northeastern.edu/paper.pdf)). Consumer Reports says other third-party contacts include apps built into the car's screen, such as navigation and roadside assistance ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/personal-information/your-car-is-sharing-data-with-big-tech-companies-study-finds-a4474820962/)). The researchers could not read the content of the cars' encrypted traffic, and could not see the cars' cellular traffic except on a Tesla Model 3, so they call their results "a lower bound" on privacy risks ([Northeastern paper](https://automatictransmission.khoury.northeastern.edu/paper.pdf)).

Consumer Reports says "28 of 30 connected mobile apps sent data to at least one outside advertising and/or analytics company, and seven sent at least one piece of personally identifiable information" ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/personal-information/your-car-is-sharing-data-with-big-tech-companies-study-finds-a4474820962/)).

Some apps sent the VIN. Consumer Reports names four GM apps (myCadillac, myChevrolet, myBuick and myGMC) plus the HondaLink, MyNissan and Lincoln apps as "sharing VINs paired with either email addresses or location data" ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/personal-information/your-car-is-sharing-data-with-big-tech-companies-study-finds-a4474820962/)). The paper explains why that matters: a VIN "cannot be changed," and paired with an email it can link a car owner to "behavioral data and purchase history from other websites and mobile apps" ([Northeastern paper](https://automatictransmission.khoury.northeastern.edu/paper.pdf)).

After the researchers showed Honda their findings, Honda told one analytics vendor, Amplitude, to delete the location data it had received, and stopped sending it ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/personal-information/your-car-is-sharing-data-with-big-tech-companies-study-finds-a4474820962/)).

## Your phone's data in the car

When you pair a phone, the car can store your contacts, call logs and texts.

Class actions against five carmakers alleged that the car "downloads all text messages and call logs" and keeps them after you delete them from your phone. In 2023 the Ninth Circuit upheld dismissal of all five. The data had stayed in the cars, and the court found the plaintiffs had not shown the injury Washington's law requires ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/cars/2023/11/five-big-carmakers-beat-lawsuits-alleging-infotainment-systems-invade-privacy/)). The court did not say storage is harmless.

Forensic tools can read it. Berla sells its vehicle tools to "law enforcement, military, civil and regulatory agencies" and others ([Berla](https://berla.co/)).

**What to do:** Before you sell, return a lease, or return a rental, delete your paired phone from the car and do a factory reset. Ford says a master reset "removes any imported personal information from the vehicle (such as contact lists, names of paired devices, or the names of connected Wi-Fi networks or hot-spots)." It tells you to reset before selling, and before and after renting ([Ford](https://web.archive.org/web/20261006015226/https://www.ford.com/help/privacy/) (archived copy, Oct 6, 2026)).

## If someone is using your car to track you

Whoever controls a car's app account may see where it is. The FCC wrote that "news reports suggest that these services have also been used to stalk, harass, and revictimize survivors of domestic violence" ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-04-23/html/2024-08642.htm)). But the FCC has only proposed rules. We found no final rule ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions%5Bterm%5D=%22connected+car+services%22&conditions%5Bagencies%5D%5B%5D=federal-communications-commission)).

California has a law. SB 1394 requires carmakers to cut off another person's remote access within two business days when a driver asks, with proof such as a title or court order ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/06/29/automakers-warn-california-vehicle-sales-could-be-halted-wednesday-if-bill-doesnt-pass/)). In June 2026, the governor signed SB 719, which delays it ([Governor of California](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/06/30/governor-newsom-signs-legislation-6-30-26/)). Repairer Driven News, citing the carmakers' trade group, reports that SB 719 moves the deadline to July 1, 2027 for model year 2027 and older cars unless that is technologically infeasible. Model years 2028 to 2030 must comply as soon as practicable after sale, unless that is technologically infeasible. All cars from model year 2031 must comply ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/07/01/california-gov-signs-bill-pushing-deadline-on-vehicle-technology-requirements/)).

Cutting off someone's access to your car's app can alert them. If you are in danger, make a safety plan with an advocate first. You can call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788 ([The Hotline](https://www.thehotline.org/)). Our [Digital Safety Kit for Stalking and Abuse Survivors](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit) explains why.

## FAQ

**Does my car share my driving data with my insurer?**

It depends on the brand. GM now needs your "affirmative consent" to share driving data with GM Insurance ([GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy)). Kia says Driving Score data may go to LexisNexis when Kia Connect is active, and may go to insurers if you join its insurance program ([Kia](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/privacy-policy.html)). Pull your Arity and LexisNexis reports to see what exists.

**How do I get my LexisNexis driving report?**

Request your Consumer Disclosure Report at [consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/request), or by phone at 1-866-897-8126 ([LexisNexis](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/consumer)). A letter arrives by mail first. LexisNexis does not say the report includes telematics. Kia's policy says Kia may send trip data there ([Kia](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/privacy-policy.html)).

**How do I stop GM/OnStar sharing my data?**

Check Privacy Settings in the car, or call 1-888-466-7827 to unenroll from OnStar. GM's statement names the "Data and Privacy" section of your GM account only for exterior camera and sensor data consent. Then ask for deletion at [gm.com/consumer-privacy](https://www.gm.com/consumer-privacy) or 1-866-697-7482 ([GM](https://www.gm.com/privacy)).

**Can driving data raise my insurance rates?**

Yes, in some states. Progressive says Snapshot rates "may increase with high-risk driving" ([Progressive](https://www.progressive.com/auto/discounts/snapshot/)). In 2024, an insurance trade-group expert told Senator Wyden's office that only Louisiana and Montana barred using telematics to raise premiums, and that California allowed it only for mileage verification. State rules may have changed since ([Wyden-Markey letter](https://www.wyden.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/wyden-markey_auto_privacy_letter_to_ftc.pdf)). In 2026, California said its drivers were not directly affected by GM's data sales, likely because state insurance law bars insurers from using driving data to set rates ([CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/05/when-it-comes-to-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-in-the-drivers-seat-attorney-general-bonta-partners-secure-12-75-million-general-motors-privacy-settlement/)).

**Does deleting the car app stop tracking?**

Usually no. Ford says deleting its app without a master reset "will not disable data sharing from the vehicle" ([Ford](https://web.archive.org/web/20261006015226/https://www.ford.com/help/privacy/) (archived copy, Oct 6, 2026)). Use the brand's off switch or phone line.

**What should I do before selling my car?**

Delete your paired phones, do a factory reset, and remove the car from your account in the brand's app. Ford says to reset before selling ([Ford](https://web.archive.org/web/20261006015226/https://www.ford.com/help/privacy/) (archived copy, Oct 6, 2026)). Nissan owners can use "Delete Vehicle & Unsubscribe" in the Owners Portal ([Nissan](https://www.nissanusa.com/connect/privacy)).
