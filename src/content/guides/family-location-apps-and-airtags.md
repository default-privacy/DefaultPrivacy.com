## Part 1: Your family's location app

### Life360

One privacy policy covers the Life360 app and Life360's Tile trackers, Pet GPS and Jiobit ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy)).

#### What it collects

Life360's policy, last modified Aug 26, 2026, lists ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy)):

- "precise geolocation data (including GPS coordinates, latitude/longitude information, and motion sensor information)."
- Device details, including "battery status, and advertising identifiers." An advertising identifier is an ID ad companies use to recognize your phone.
- Driving and movement: "speed, altitude, estimated number of steps and/or distance covered, and heading."

Drive Detection flags "phone usage, high speed, hard braking, and rapid acceleration," and those events "are then shared with the entire Circle" ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053499870487-Drive-Detection-Analysis)). A Circle is Life360's name for a group of people who share location.

Life360 asks iPhone users to set location to "Always" with Precise Location on. Its permissions page ends: "Location data may be shared with partners for crash detection, research, analytics, attribution, and tailored advertising" ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053398402839-Life360-Permission-Settings)).

#### Does Life360 sell location?

Its policy says it licenses it. The state-law section reads ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy)):

> "TEXAS NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data."

A few lines later:

> "As described above, we license certain information, including precise geolocation data (which is "sensitive" personal data under certain State Privacy Laws) to select business partners for their own advertising and monetization purposes."

The policy says it does not hand over your name, email or phone number for this, "but may share hashed versions of them with our partners" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy)). Hashing turns an email into a code. Two companies holding the same email get the same code, so it can still match you across databases.

Life360's Q2 2026 results say its "other revenue," which includes data revenue, rose 25% to $11.6 million. Life360 credits higher data revenue, "primarily attributable to increased data volumes resulting from user growth," and higher partnership revenue ([Life360 Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1581760/000158176026000142/life360incq226mediarelea.htm)).

#### What the app shows vs what Life360 keeps

The app shows your Circle "Free - 2 days, Silver - 7 days, Gold/Platinum - 30 days" of history ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053602225559-Location-Drive-History)).

Life360's law enforcement guidelines, updated Oct 2, 2026, describe a longer record ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16369337969431-Life360-Law-Enforcement-Guidelines)):

> "Life360 generally retains location data in the form of dwell data for up to 18 months. Raw location data is retained for approximately 30 days, with the exception of the last place a Tile was recorded."

Dwell data is "locations where a member did not move for at least 15-20 minutes and the approximate length of time in such locations" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16369337969431-Life360-Law-Enforcement-Guidelines)). In plain terms, that can include places like home, school, work, a clinic or a friend's house.

- History follows the person. "Location history belongs to your account, not a specific Circle." If you join a new Circle, its members "may be able to see your location history" ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053602225559-Location-Drive-History)). This matters for separated parents.
- Turning off sharing with a Circle does not stop collection. Life360 says it "will continue to collect your location information," including "to provide location-based advertising" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16044136535703-Limit-Location-Sharing-with-Your-Circle-s)).

#### Arity, the Allstate company in the app

Arity is a driving-data company owned by Allstate. It powers Life360's driving features. Life360 shares driving event data with it and says it "may share geolocation and movement data," device identifiers and registration details ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16044058241559-Driving-Analytics-Services)).

Life360's page says Arity builds "a driving risk score," and: "For US members only, Arity may use this information to provide personalized content including targeted advertising, offer discounts, rewards or pricing offers by third parties such as insurance companies, and for other commercial purposes" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16044058241559-Driving-Analytics-Services)).

Disconnecting Arity has a cost. Life360 says: "Drive Detection, including Crash Alerts, Driving reports, and Drive history, is powered by Arity. These features will be disabled if data sharing with Arity is turned off" ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053499870487-Drive-Detection-Analysis)).

Life360's Arity page says you "may be able to limit some of this data sharing with Arity" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16044058241559-Driving-Analytics-Services)). It doesn't say which parts. To opt out of Arity's processing completely, Life360 says to contact Customer Support ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053658294807-Crash-Detection-Data-Collection)).

#### Ad partners

Life360 lists its in-app ad partners as "Arity, Google Ad Manager, Meta Ads Manager, Pubmatic, Smaato, Magnite, Relevant Digital" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/39882987162775-Life360-App-Ad-serving-Partners)).

#### Kids and teens

The policy says, word for word ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy)):

> "We do not share or sell personal information of members we know to be under 18, nor do we show ads in Child or Teen accounts."

The sentence just before it says: "Life360 may disclose the personal information of Children and Teens to our service providers and our business partners that offer integrated services."

The state-law section adds: "We do not engage in sales, sharing, or targeted advertising using personal information about anyone we know to be under the age of 18" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy)).

Arity provides an integrated service (driving and crash detection). So here is a question we could not get answered from Life360's documents: **can Arity use a known teen's driving data for its own commercial purposes?** The policy doesn't say. We are not saying Life360 sells kids' data. We are saying the wording leaves this open.

**Practical tip: enter your child's real birthday.** The rule covers members Life360 "know[s] to be under 18." Life360 learns age from the "Enter your birthday" step at signup ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053525850519-Create-a-Life360-Account)). A teen on an adult birthday gets adult treatment.

#### Opt out: each adult, on their own phone

Life360 says Your Privacy Choices "is limited to the data for the account logged in at the time of the request (i.e., it's not relevant to other circle members)" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16043995801623-Limit-Information-Sharing-with-Third-party-Partners-Through-In-App-Privacy-Settings)). Every adult in the Circle has to do this.

1. Open Life360. Tap **Settings** in the upper left, then **Privacy Center** ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053465742743-Privacy-Center)).
2. Tap **Your Privacy Choices**. Turn off the **Include my data** toggle. Life360 says this "also includes Traffic and retail insights" ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053465742743-Privacy-Center)).
3. Check it anyway: tap **Traffic and retail insights** and make sure **Include my data** is off there too ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053465742743-Privacy-Center)).
4. In Your Privacy Choices, set **Personalized Ads** the way you want ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16043995801623-Limit-Information-Sharing-with-Third-party-Partners-Through-In-App-Privacy-Settings)). On iPhone, choosing "Ask App Not to Track" also opts out ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16043995801623-Limit-Information-Sharing-with-Third-party-Partners-Through-In-App-Privacy-Settings)).
5. Optional, with a cost: under **Trusted Partners**, tap **Arity**, turn off **Connect to Arity**, then tap **Disconnect Arity** ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053465742743-Privacy-Center)). This turns off crash detection. Make it a deliberate choice.

What the opt-out leaves running: Life360 says it "will continue to collect your device location and movement data and to share such information with your Circles and with third parties as needed to support and provide the Life360 Services," but "will no longer share your personal information with those third party partners for their own commercial purposes" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16043995801623-Limit-Information-Sharing-with-Third-party-Partners-Through-In-App-Privacy-Settings)).

**The website toggle doesn't cover the app.** Life360's "Do Not Sell or Share" toggle on its websites "is a web-based choice only, and does not affect app-based personal information sales or sharing" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16043972507287-To-Opt-out-of-Life360-Sharing-Data-with-Third-parties-for-Advertising-Purposes)). Use the in-app steps.

#### Deleting your history

You can't delete history on its own. Life360 says: "At this time, there is no way to delete your history or driving events. If you choose to delete your entire account, your history and driving events will be deleted" ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053602225559-Location-Drive-History)).

To delete the account: **Settings** > **Account** > **Delete Account**, confirm, then follow the emailed link ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053748967319-Delete-My-Account)). Deleting the account does not cancel a paid membership ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053748967319-Delete-My-Account)). Cancel that separately.

#### The lawsuits

- **Texas v. Allstate and Arity.** Filed by the Texas Attorney General in Montgomery County. In April 2025 a Texas judge dismissed Allstate Corp. and Arity 875 LLC from the case for lack of jurisdiction ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/allstate-ends-texas-data-collection-claims-in-latest-paxton-blow)). Allstate's filing for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 still says the company "is subject to" the Texas lawsuit ([Allstate 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/899051/000089905126000118/all-20260630.htm)).
- **Federal class action against Allstate and Arity.** A class action in Illinois alleges Arity's code in apps including Life360 collected driving data. In March 2026 the court kept the wiretap claims and rejected Allstate's argument that federal credit-reporting law blocks the state-law claims. It dismissed counts based on rate increases that didn't seek statutory damages. Life360 is named as one of the apps. It is not a defendant ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/03/19/court-allows-suit-alleging-allstate-collected-and-sold-consumer-data-to-move-forward-dismisses-portion/)).

These are allegations. No court has ruled on whether the data collection was unlawful.

### Apple Find My (sharing with people)

Apple's Find My privacy page says ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/find-my/)):

> "If you and your friend are both on iOS 17 or later, your location will not be accessible to Apple."

It adds that with someone on an older system, your location "may be accessible to Apple if it is actively requested." And: "Your location is retained for up to 24 hours in order to provide the service, after which it is deleted."

Apple still knows who you share with. The same page lists "The people you have shared with" among the data it uses.

**To see and stop sharing** ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105104)):

- One person: Find My > **People** > tap the person > **More** > **Stop Sharing My Location**. Apple says that person "won't be notified."
- Everyone: Find My > **Me** > turn off **Share My Location**. Turning it back on restores everyone you shared with before.
- Everything at once: **Settings** > **Privacy & Security** > **Safety Check** ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/how-safety-check-works-ips2aad835e1/web)).

Deleting the Find My app does nothing: "people that you've shared your location with can still see your location" ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105104)).

### Google location sharing

Google says location sharing is off by default. People you share with see your name, photo, real-time location and battery, and may see "where you've recently been" ([Google](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/9363497?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)).

On a Messages help page, Google contrasts end-to-end encrypted messages, which "can only be read by people in your chat," with location sharing, which "is powered by Maps and processes your location to provide the location sharing feature" ([Google](https://support.google.com/messages/answer/16929688?hl=en)).

We found no Google page that says how long live location sharing data is kept, or whether it is used for ads.

**To see and stop sharing:**

- Android: **Settings** > **Location** > **Location services** > **Google Location Sharing** > tap the list of people > **Stop** next to a name ([Google](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/9363497?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)).
- Computer: myaccount.google.com > **People & sharing** > **Location Sharing** > **Manage Location Sharing** > pick the person > **Stop** ([Google](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/7326816?hl=en)).
- Kids on supervised accounts: Google says "Location Sharing works differently if a parent manages your Google Account" ([Google](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/9363497?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)).

### Social maps

- **Instagram Map.** Meta says "Location sharing is off unless you opt in." Parents with supervision set up "will receive a notification if your teen starts sharing their location." Location-tagged posts can still appear on the map ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/08/new-instagram-features-help-you-connect/)).
- **Snap Map.** Snap says "Location sharing on Snap Map is off by default." To hide, open Snap Map, tap the gear, and pick **Ghost Mode** ([Snapchat](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/7012309470740-How-do-I-share-my-location-on-Snap-Map)).

We couldn't find a Meta page that says how long it keeps Map location.

### The law, briefly

South Carolina's Act 96, signed Feb 5, 2026, says: "Precise geolocation information of minors cannot be collected by default unless necessary to the provision of the covered online service. An obvious notice to the minor must be provided when precise geolocation information is being collected or used" ([SC Legislature](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/3431.htm)).

NetChoice, a tech industry group, has challenged it in federal court. As of its page's last update, no ruling was posted ([NetChoice](https://netchoice.org/netchoice-v-wilson-south-carolina/)).

## Part 2: A tracker that isn't yours

### How phone alerts work

An item tracker is a small Bluetooth tag, like an AirTag, that reports its location through nearby phones. In May 2024 Apple and Google shipped a shared system so phones warn you about a tag moving with you ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/05/apple-and-google-deliver-support-for-unwanted-tracking-alerts-in-ios-and-android/)).

**iPhone.** Apple says to update to iOS 17.5 or later, which is needed for alerts about non-Apple tags. The alert reads "[Item] Found Moving With You." Apple's checklist ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874)):

- Location Services on, and under **System Services**, **Significant Locations** on.
- Bluetooth on.
- **Settings** > **Notifications** > **Tracking Notifications** > **Allow Notifications** on.
- Airplane Mode off. "If your device is in Airplane Mode, you won't receive tracking notifications."

Past alerts are listed at the bottom of Find My > **Items**. An alert fires when a tag "is separated from its owner and seen moving with you over time" ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874)).

**Android.** Needs Android 6 or later. You can scan on demand: **Settings** > **Safety & Emergency** > **Unknown tracker alerts** > **Scan now** ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)). Google says it works with "Find Hub network compatible tags, headphones, and Apple AirTags," and that you get "one alert per tracker each day" ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)).

### What the alerts miss

- **Tile.** Tile is owned by Life360 ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16369337969431-Life360-Law-Enforcement-Guidelines)). Google's list of trackers its alerts work with doesn't include Tile ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)). Apple's list of tag makers that committed to the shared alerts doesn't include Tile either ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/05/apple-and-google-deliver-support-for-unwanted-tracking-alerts-in-ios-and-android/)). We found no Tile or Life360 statement that Tiles trigger the iPhone or Android alerts. Tile's help page offers its own manual **Scan and Secure** in the Tile app ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582987898135-How-to-Handle-Unwanted-Tracking)). It takes up to 10 minutes and you have to walk or drive away from where you started ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582996499095-Tile-Scan-and-Secure-Overview)). And it "will not detect Tile's in Anti-Theft Mode" ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582987898135-How-to-Handle-Unwanted-Tracking)).
- **Tile's Anti-Theft Mode** hides an owner's Tiles from Scan and Secure. It requires ID and a selfie scan ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582923425815-Tile-Anti-Theft-Mode)). The often-quoted $1 million is in Life360's terms: misuse makes you "liable to Life360/Tile for liquidated damages in the amount of $1 million" ([Life360 terms](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/17865862545815-Life360-Product-and-Services-Terms)). Liquidated damages are a sum set in a contract, owed to Tile. The same terms say an owner's information "may be shared with law enforcement" if their Tiles are suspected of criminal use. If you think a Tile in Anti-Theft Mode is tracking you, Tile says its Customer Care team can help, and police can get the owner's details with a court order ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582987898135-How-to-Handle-Unwanted-Tracking)).
- **Samsung Galaxy SmartTags.** Unconfirmed. Samsung's own detection is in the SmartThings app and "can only detect smart tags that are compatible with SmartThings Find" ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/ae/support/mobile-devices/how-to-disable-smarttag-2-location-tracking/)). We found no Samsung statement on whether its tags trigger the iPhone or Android alerts.
- **Silenced AirTags still show up on your phone.** iFixit found the AirTag 2's speaker can be disabled and the tag keeps working ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/05/ifixit-tears-down-new-airtag-finds-50-louder-speaker-still-100-easy-to-disable/)). That removes the beep. Apple says the phone alert is a separate warning from the sound ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874)).
- **Shared AirTags.** When an AirTag is shared with you in Find My, "unwanted tracking notifications for that AirTag are suppressed for all sharing group members" ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/stop-sharing-an-airtag-ips0c073d231/web)). If you're in a sharing group you didn't choose, remove yourself. Apple suggests checking whether the AirTag is near you first, because once you leave you can no longer see where it is ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/stop-sharing-an-airtag-ips0c073d231/web)).

### If you find one

Think about safety before you disable it. The owner may notice it has gone quiet.

- **Find it.** On iPhone, tap the alert, then **Continue** and **Play Sound**, or **Find Nearby** on newer models ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874)).
- **Photograph where you found it.** Tile says that if it's safe to move the device, you may need to "document (with a photo or video) where you found the device" ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582950641559-Tile-Scan-and-Secure-FAQ)).
- **Get the serial number.** Hold the top of an iPhone near the tag, then tap the notification. It can show the serial number and part of the registered owner's phone number or email ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/detect-unwanted-trackers-ips139b15fd9/web)). On Android, hold it to the back of the phone ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)).
- **Know what disabling does.** Apple: "After the item is disabled, the owner can no longer get updates on its current location" ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/detect-unwanted-trackers-ips139b15fd9/web)). Google adds the owner "may still be able to check the last location" ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)). Google also warns some trackers reset when turned off, so police "can't get additional information about these trackers, like who its owner is" ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)). Get the serial number first.
- **Police.** Apple says police "can work with Apple to request information related to the item," and you may need the tag and its serial number ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874)).

The National Network to End Domestic Violence (NNEDV) warns that "Making changes will often alert the other person, and they might become more abusive," and that changes "might also erase evidence" ([NNEDV](https://www.techsafety.org/location-tracking)). It recommends keeping a log of every incident ([NNEDV](https://www.techsafety.org/documentationtips)).

If this is part of a wider pattern, start with our [Digital Safety Kit for Stalking and Abuse Survivors](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit). It covers safety planning, accounts and devices in order.

### Who can see your own tags

- **Apple** says "only the owner of a device can access its location data," and "No one, including Apple, knows the identity or location of any device that helped find it" ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/01/apple-introduces-new-airtag-with-expanded-range-and-improved-findability/)). An owner can also share an AirTag with others, who then see it too ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/stop-sharing-an-airtag-ips0c073d231/web)).
- **Google** says tag locations are end-to-end encrypted and "Google cannot decrypt, see, or otherwise use the location data" ([Google](https://security.googleblog.com/2024/04/find-my-device-network-security-privacy-protections.html)).
- **Tile.** Georgia Tech researchers reported in September 2025 that Tile's location reports lack end-to-end encryption, so Tile's servers "persistently learn the location of all users and tags." They also found Scan and Secure records Anti-Theft Tiles and only hides them from the results, so "A user with a modified app can easily circumvent the Anti-Theft mode" ([Georgia Tech, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00350v1)). These are the researchers' findings. Life360 told The Register it had "made a number of improvements" since the report, without details ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/30/tile_trackers_unencrypted_info/)). Tile's security page, dated Oct 3, 2025, says Tile encrypts location "in transit and on the internal servers at rest" and does "not sell your Tile location data" ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30583010520087-Tile-Security)). Encryption at rest protects stored data from outsiders. It doesn't stop the company holding the keys from reading it, which matches what the researchers reported.

### The AirTag lawsuits

- In early 2026 Judge Vince Chhabria, a federal judge in California, denied class status in the main AirTag stalking case, as reported by Top Class Actions. Plaintiffs then filed individual suits ([Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/apple-airtag-stalking-claims-move-to-individual-lawsuits-after-class-action-fails/)).
- On Oct 2, 2026, a panel of federal judges sent 17 AirTag stalking suits from other districts to one proceeding, MDL No. 3199, before Judge Noel Wise in Northern California ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73743430/in-re-apple-airtag-marketing-sales-practices-and-products-liability/)).
- In an Aug 25, 2026 brief, Apple said it had settled "20 actions in the last two months," which The Mac Observer counts as 15 federal suits, 4 state suits and 1 claim settled before a suit was filed. Terms weren't disclosed ([The Mac Observer](https://www.macobserver.com/news/apple-settled-20-airtag-stalking-lawsuits-two-months/)).

No court has found Apple liable. The claims are allegations, and Apple is fighting them.

### Tracking your own family

The makers say these tags are for things.

- Apple: AirTag is "Designed exclusively for tracking objects, and not people or pets" ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/01/apple-introduces-new-airtag-with-expanded-range-and-improved-findability/)).
- Tile: "designed for locating lost or misplaced items, not for tracking people" ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582996499095-Tile-Scan-and-Secure-Overview)).
- Chipolo advises "against using them to track people," children included. It points to limited Bluetooth range and beeping when a tag is away from its owner, and suggests "dedicated child safety wearables" instead ([Chipolo](https://support.chipolo.net/hc/en-us/articles/20519566961181-Can-I-use-Chipolo-to-track-my-kids)).

One practical catch: an AirTag in the bag of a child or parent who carries a phone can set off alerts on their phone. Sharing the AirTag with them in Find My stops that, and they'll know it's there ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/stop-sharing-an-airtag-ips0c073d231/web)).

## FAQ

**Does Life360 sell my location?**

Life360's policy says "We may sell your sensitive personal data" in its Texas notice, and that it licenses "precise geolocation data" to "select business partners for their own advertising and monetization purposes" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16038777217175-Life360-Privacy-Policy)). You can opt out in the app under Settings > Privacy Center > Your Privacy Choices. Each adult has to do it on their own account.

**Can Life360 see my location history after I delete it?**

You can't delete history on its own. Only deleting your account deletes it ([Life360](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053602225559-Location-Drive-History)). The app shows 2 to 30 days, but Life360's law enforcement guidelines say it "generally retains location data in the form of dwell data for up to 18 months" ([Life360](https://legal.corp.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/16369337969431-Life360-Law-Enforcement-Guidelines)).

**Will my iPhone tell me if someone put an AirTag on me?**

Yes, if it is on iOS 17.5 or later with Bluetooth, Location Services, Significant Locations and Tracking Notifications on, and Airplane Mode off ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119874)). It won't warn you about an AirTag shared with you through Find My ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/stop-sharing-an-airtag-ips0c073d231/web)).

**Does my phone detect a Tile?**

Probably not. Google's alert list doesn't include Tile ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13658562)), and we found no Tile statement that Tiles trigger phone alerts. Tile points people to its own Scan and Secure in the Tile app, which can't see Tiles in Anti-Theft Mode ([Tile](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/30582987898135-How-to-Handle-Unwanted-Tracking)).

**Is Find My location sharing private?**

Apple says that when you and the other person both run iOS 17 or later, your location "will not be accessible to Apple," and it is kept for up to 24 hours ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/find-my/)). Apple still knows who you share with. The people you share with see where you are, so review the list in Find My > People.

**Should I put an AirTag on my kid?**

Apple says AirTag is for "tracking objects, and not people or pets" ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/01/apple-introduces-new-airtag-with-expanded-range-and-improved-findability/)). Chipolo recommends a dedicated child wearable instead ([Chipolo](https://support.chipolo.net/hc/en-us/articles/20519566961181-Can-I-use-Chipolo-to-track-my-kids)). If your child has a phone, the location sharing options in Part 1 are built for people.
