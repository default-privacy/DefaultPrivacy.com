Set up an email alias and a second number first, then a mail address that isn't your home before you move. Give that address to the people who send you mail, and skip the permanent USPS change of address. Then keep the new home address off utility, voter and property records where you lawfully can, and file people-search opt-outs after you arrive. Prevention comes first because cleanup never finishes: the sites refill from new records.

No step makes you invisible. Each one below says what it does, where it stops and how to tell it's done.

---

## If you are leaving an abuser, read this first

Changes can alert the person you are leaving. Changing a password, signing a device out or closing an account can be noticed, so plan each visible change with a trained advocate, ideally before you do it. In the US, the [National Domestic Violence Hotline](https://www.thehotline.org/) is free, confidential and open 24/7: call 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788. Do the steps below from a device and account the person has never had access to. The [stalking and abuse survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit) covers that order.

Step 5 (state Address Confidentiality Programs) is built for you. Read it before the others.

## The legal line

Privacy aliases are fine: a mailbox address, an email alias, a second phone number. False names or false addresses on government, bank, lease or credit paperwork are not.

- The mail-agent form you sign for a mailbox says that anyone who gives false or misleading information on it may face criminal and civil penalties ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).
- Federal law makes it a crime to knowingly and willfully make a materially false statement in a matter within the jurisdiction of the federal executive, legislative or judicial branch ([18 U.S.C. 1001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1001)).
- US banks must get a residential or business street address from an individual before opening an account, with exceptions for people who have no street address ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)).

So give your bank and federal agencies your real details. For landlords and lenders, check what they require, and never give them false details. The plan below limits who else gets your address.

## Step 1: Set up an email alias and a second number first

**What to do.** Before anything else, set up an [email alias](/guides/email-aliases-simplelogin-vs-addy) and a [second phone number](/guides/get-a-second-phone-number). The mailbox form in Step 2 asks for a phone number and an email address ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)), and so do utility sign-ups, store accounts, deliveries and apartment applications. Give the new ones wherever your real ones aren't required. Your name, email, phone and address together are what let a broker tie a new record to you.

**The limit.** Your bank and federal agencies (see the legal section) need your real details. Check what your landlord requires. An alias protects you from the rest.

**How you know it's done.** Your new sign-ups use the alias and the second number, and your real ones are kept for the few places that require them.

## Step 2: Get a mail address before you move

**What to do.** Rent a mailbox that isn't your home, in any city, while you still live at your old address. Two kinds:

- **Private mailbox or virtual mailbox** (a "commercial mail receiving agency"). Examples in our directory: [Anytime Mailbox](/directory/anytime-mailbox). You sign USPS [PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf). It needs two IDs: one government-issued photo ID, and a second that confirms the home address you list on the form. You sign, or confirm your signature, in front of the mailbox company's staff or a notary, in person or on live video. The form says to attach copies of both IDs. Before you pay, confirm the current rules on [usps.com](https://www.usps.com/).
- **USPS PO Box.** You need two forms of ID, and USPS says the application isn't fully approved until it verifies your current permanent physical address ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). If you are moving, it says you can apply online or in person before you go. You start verification at a Post Office where you live now, then complete it again at your new address after you move.

**The limit.** The mailbox company and USPS hold your real home address. USPS says it doesn't disclose that information to third parties without your consent except in listed cases, including law enforcement, auditors and agents it uses to do its work, and says information about someone who files a protective court order with the application won't be disclosed except under a court order ([PS Form 1583, Privacy Act statement](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). Give your bank your real home address; don't assume it will accept a mailbox address.

**How you know it's done.** You have the street address and box number, the company has your approved form on file, and a test letter reaches you.

## Step 3: Skip the permanent USPS change of address

**What to do.** Give your mail address yourself to the people and companies that mail you things: shops, subscriptions, doctors, friends. Give your bank, your employer and federal agencies your real home address. Do this instead of filing a permanent change of address with USPS. If you still need a safety net for the weeks while senders catch up, file a temporary one and point it at the mailbox, not your new home. USPS says "You may file a COA request to a CMRA" (a commercial mail receiving agency, meaning a private mailbox) ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). That way the forwarding order lists the mailbox, not your new home. If you are enrolling in a state Address Confidentiality Program, don't file one at all until you have asked the program (see Step 5).

**Why.** USPS describes NCOALink as a dataset of about 160 million permanent change-of-address records, built from names and addresses of people who filed with USPS, and says the data goes regularly to companies it has licensed ([USPS PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/mailing-and-shipping-services/NCOALink)). Mailers use it to update their lists before mailing. A permanent filing adds your new address to that dataset. A temporary request still gives USPS the address you point it at, which is why it should be the mailbox.

**The limit.** USPS's technical guide for licensees says temporary requests are only in the 48-month product, the one sold to its Full Service Provider licensees, and that a match to one comes back with no new address ([NCOALink User Technical Reference Guide](https://postalpro.usps.com/mnt/glusterfs/2023-04/usertechinfo_v12.pdf)). So a temporary request can still tell some mailers that you moved, just not where. Treat it as the better option, not a guarantee. Temporary forwarding has a time limit, with a 15-day minimum. USPS's FAQ says up to 185 days, and a second request can extend it by up to 179 more, for 364 in total ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). Mail from businesses, banks and agencies that use ancillary endorsements (mailer instructions for undeliverable mail) may not be forwarded (USPS gives driver's licenses and bank statements as examples), so update those senders directly ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). Any change of address, temporary or permanent, also makes USPS mail a Move Validation Letter to the address you are leaving ([USPS FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). If you are leaving someone who still lives or gets mail there, that letter can alert them, so plan it with an advocate before you file (see the [stalking and abuse survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit)).

**How you know it's done.** The people and companies that mail you things show the new mail address, your bank and employer have your real home address, and no permanent change of address is on file.

## Step 4: Utilities, lease and deliveries

**What to do.** Where a form asks for an address, give the service address only if the service needs it. Ask each utility and your landlord whether bills and notices can go to your mailbox address and whether they share customer data. Order packages to the mailbox or a pickup point where you can.

**The limit.** Your service address and lease are real records. Whether a given utility or landlord shares them is theirs to say, and we found no primary source that covers all of them, so ask and keep the answer.

**How you know it's done.** Bills come to the mailbox address, and you have a written answer about data sharing from each.

## Step 5: Voter registration, driver's license and state Address Confidentiality Programs

**What to do.** You have to register to vote and get a license where you live, so this step is about limiting who can read them. Voter files are government records and are often subject to public records laws, and the US Election Assistance Commission says some of this information may be publicly available, though most states prohibit commercial use of the voter file ([EAC](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Voter_Roll_Privacy.pdf)). Ask your state election office what its voter file shows and whether you can mark your record confidential.

If you are at risk from stalking, domestic violence, harassment or rape, apply to your state's Address Confidentiality Program (ACP). The National Association of Secretaries of State says ACPs give participants a free, forwardable substitute address or a PO box to use instead of the real one, that in most states it can be used for driver's licenses, school enrollment, voter registration and other public records, and that applicants usually enroll through a counselor, often at a victim assistance center ([NASS, October 2022](https://www.nass.org/sites/default/files/reports/NASS-White-Paper-Voters-ACP-Programs-Oct22.pdf)).

Ask your program before you file any USPS change of address, including a temporary one. Oregon's program tells participants "DO NOT submit a change of address or mail forwarding request with the United States Postal Service" and tells new enrollees to "Cancel any USPS mail forwarding service you have set up" ([Oregon DOJ ACP Participant Guide, updated 2024](https://www.doj.state.or.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/ACP-Participant-Guide.pdf)). Other programs may differ, so ask yours.

**The limit.** Programs differ by state, and not every state has one. The lists we found disagree, so check your state's own program page. Some states make ACP voters register under their real address and flag the record confidential instead ([NASS](https://www.nass.org/sites/default/files/reports/NASS-White-Paper-Voters-ACP-Programs-Oct22.pdf)). An ACP doesn't stop a landlord, bank or utility from knowing where you live.

**How you know it's done.** You have your ACP card and substitute address, or your voter record is marked confidential, you've checked how the state's records show you, and you've asked your program before filing any USPS change of address.

## Step 6: Property records

**What to do.** If you buy, the deed is recorded with the county. Lawfare notes that people-search sites commonly get their data from government records such as voting registries and property filings, and that the county keeps the original even if a site deletes its copy ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/people-search-data-brokers-stalking-and-publicly-available-information-carve-outs)). Before you sign, ask a local real estate attorney what your state lets you shield and how a trust or LLC would affect your mortgage and insurance. If you rent, you mostly avoid this step.

**The limit.** Any shield is a state and county question, and it can change what a lender or insurer asks for. Don't put a false name on a deed.

**How you know it's done.** You have the attorney's answer in writing and you've looked up your own address on the county's property search.

## Step 7: Opt out of people-search sites, after you move

**What to do.** Wait until your new address has had time to appear, then search your name and the new address on the major people-search sites and file each site's opt-out. Our [broker removal guide](/guides/remove-name-from-data-brokers) has the steps, and [keeping your address off public records](/guides/keep-home-address-off-public-records) covers the rest. Paid options in our directory include [Incogni](/directory/incogni) and [Optery](/directory/optery). The [delete yourself from the internet stack](/stacks/delete-yourself-from-the-internet) lists the wider cleanup.

If you now live in California, the state's Delete Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP) sends one deletion request to over 600 registered data brokers. Brokers began processing requests on August 1, 2026 and must check for new requests at least every 45 days, and it can take up to 90 days for status updates ([California Privacy Protection Agency](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop/)). It is open to California residents only.

**The limit.** Records that come from government files stay public, and sites re-list you when new data arrives. Opt-outs are a recurring job.

**How you know it's done.** A search for your name and new address shows nothing on the major sites, and you have a reminder to check again. Our [broker removal guide](/guides/remove-name-from-data-brokers) suggests every 90 days.

## FAQ

**Should I file a USPS change of address when I move?**

Not a permanent one, if the goal is to keep the new address away from mailers. USPS says permanent change-of-address records feed NCOALink, which it licenses to mailers ([USPS PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/mailing-and-shipping-services/NCOALink)). Update your important senders directly instead.

**What do I need for a virtual mailbox?**

PS Form 1583, a government photo ID, a second ID that confirms the address on the form, and a signature witnessed by the mailbox company or a notary ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).

**Can I get a PO Box in the new city before I move?**

Yes, if a box is available. USPS says that if you are moving you can apply online or in person, start verification at a Post Office where you live now, and complete it again at your new address after you move ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)).

**Is it legal to use a mailbox address instead of my home?**

Yes, for mail, using the form above with your real details. Banks must get a residential or business street address before opening an account, with narrow exceptions ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)), so give your bank your real home address.

**Will opting out remove me for good?**

No. Government-record sources stay public and sites refill. See the limits in Step 7.
