Your home address is sitting on people-search sites and data-broker profiles. Fixing it takes **two separate jobs**, not one.

## The Short Answer

Fixing it takes two jobs: scrub what is already public, then stop handing the address out again.

1. Remove: file opt-outs at people-search sites and data brokers yourself, or pay a removal service to file them.
2. Prevent: use a mail receiving address on the next public form, and turn on privacy protection on any domain you register.
3. Check again, because brokers re-list when new data arrives. One pass is not a finish.

Removal will not make you untraceable. It will not hide you from a bank, the IRS, or a court with valid process.

## Two jobs, not one

**1. Scrub what's already out there**

File opt-outs at people-search sites and data brokers, then **keep checking** — they re-list when new data arrives.

**2. Stop handing the address out again**

Use a mail receiving address where a form asks for a public one. Do not put the house on a domain registration, a public profile, or a listing you control.

Do only the first and the leaks keep refilling. Do only the second and old records stay searchable.

## Where your address becomes public

### Data brokers

Aggregators buy and resell name, address, and phone profiles. Hundreds of broker sites feed people-search products. Opt-outs work, then **expire** when brokers refresh their data.

### People-search sites

Consumer "find anyone" pages republish broker data. Removal is ongoing, not one-and-done.

### Records you publish yourself

A domain registration, a public social profile, a listing, or a form that prints the house address becomes a source brokers harvest. Privacy protection on the domain, and a separate mail address on anything public, stops the next copy.

---

## What removal does and doesn't do

**Does:**

- Make a casual search less likely to show the house
- Clear listings that are already indexed, if you follow through on the opt-outs
- Matter for anyone who does not want strangers at the door

**Does not:**

- Make you untraceable
- Hide you from a bank, the IRS, or a court with valid process
- Erase a record that a government office is required to keep

## FAQ

**If I opt out once, is the old address gone?**

Not for long. Brokers refresh from other sources. You still have to stop publishing the address, and you still have to check back.

**Is a separate mail address enough?**

It helps on the next form. Brokers may still link the house from older records until you opt out and keep watching.
