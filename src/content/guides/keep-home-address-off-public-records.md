Your home address is sitting on people-search sites and data-broker profiles. Fixing it takes **two separate jobs**, not one.

## The Short Answer

Fixing it takes two jobs: scrub what is already public, then stop handing the address out again.

1. Remove: file opt-outs at people-search sites and data brokers, or pay a removal service.
2. Prevent: use a mail receiving address on the next public form, and turn on privacy protection on any domain you register.
3. Check again, because brokers re-list when new data arrives. One pass is not a finish.
4. Own a home? Opt-outs don't reach the county deed. Ask a local real estate attorney what your state lets you shield.

Removal will not make you untraceable. It will not hide you from a bank, the IRS, or a court with valid process.

## Two jobs, not one

**1. Scrub what's already out there**

File opt-outs at people-search sites and data brokers, then **keep checking**. They re-list when new data arrives.

**2. Stop handing the address out again**

Use a mail receiving address where a form asks for a public one. Do not put the house on a domain registration, a public profile, or a listing you control.

Do only the first and the leaks keep refilling. Do only the second and old records stay searchable.

## Where your address becomes public

### Data brokers

Aggregators buy and resell name, address, and phone profiles. Hundreds of broker sites feed people-search products. Opt-outs work, then **expire** when brokers refresh their data.

### People-search sites

Consumer "find anyone" pages republish broker data. Removal is ongoing, not one-and-done.

### Records you publish yourself

A domain registration, a public social profile, a listing, or a form that prints the house address becomes a source brokers harvest. Privacy protection on the domain, and a separate mail address on anything public, stops the next copy.

## Property records: where your name sits next to your address

If you own your home, the county keeps the deed, and the deed carries your name. Los Angeles County says "deeds are public record in California", that its property records "are filed by grantor name, grantee name and year of recording", and that "Records deemed to be public cannot be masked or removed from the database, unless ordered by the Court" ([LA County Registrar-Recorder](https://www.lavote.gov/home/records/real-estate-records/faqs)). Florida requires each county recorder to post an index of recorded documents "on a publicly available Internet website", and the index lists "grantor and grantee names" ([Fla. Stat. 28.2221](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0028/Sections/0028.2221.html)). In a sale, the grantor is the seller and the grantee is the buyer.

The property appraiser or assessor and the tax collector keep their own records too, and in Florida those can carry the owner's name (see the Florida law below). Opt-outs don't reach any of these. The FTC says the public records these sites use "may include property records", and that "opting out of people search sites doesn't delete your information from public records, so someone could find public records about you on a government website" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)).

If you are buying soon, ask before the contract, while the deal can still change. Step 6 of the [moving guide](/guides/move-without-landing-on-people-search-sites) and Step 7 of the [start-over stack](/stacks/start-over-privately) cover the timing. Never put a false name on a deed.

### Who can get their name taken off

Some states shield certain people. In Florida, the home addresses of groups such as "active or former sworn law enforcement personnel" and judges are currently exempt. If one of them sends a "written and notarized request", the property appraiser and tax collector must remove their name "from all publicly available records maintained by the property appraiser or tax collector". The street address and legal description of the property stay. The law also says the shielded information "may be disclosed" to title insurers, title agents and attorneys in good standing with The Florida Bar. The exemption on a home address in the county's Official Records lasts "only during the period when a protected party resides at the dwelling location" ([Fla. Stat. 119.071](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0119/Sections/0119.071.html)).

Some Address Confidentiality Programs (ACPs, state programs that give people at risk a substitute address) reach property records. In Minnesota, "a program participant must submit a real property notice in writing to the county recorder", and "Only one parcel of real property may be included in each notice" ([Minn. Stat. 13.045](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/13.045)). Programs differ by state, and some can't change records made before you enrolled. ### Thinking of a trust or LLC?

Ask a local real estate attorney before you sign how it would affect your deed, mortgage and insurance. Mortgage rules matter here: Fannie Mae says it buys mortgages "made to borrowers who are natural persons", with listed exceptions for some trusts ([Fannie Mae Selling Guide B2-2-01](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-2-01/general-borrower-eligibility-requirements)).

An anonymous LLC is business privacy. It does not hide you from your bank, the IRS or a court ([what an anonymous LLC does not do](/guides/what-anonymous-llc-does-not-do)).

## What removal does and doesn't do

**Does:**

- Make a casual search less likely to show the house
- Clear listings that are already indexed, if you follow through on the opt-outs
- Matter for anyone who does not want strangers at the door

**Does not:**

- Make you untraceable
- Hide you from a bank, the IRS, or a court with valid process
- Erase a record that a government office is required to keep
- Take your name off a county deed or tax record. Opt-outs don't reach those.

## FAQ

**If I opt out once, is the old address gone?**

Not for long. Brokers refresh from other sources. You still have to stop publishing the address, and you still have to check back.

**Is a separate mail address enough?**

It helps on the next form. Brokers may still link the house from older records until you opt out and keep watching.

**Can I keep my name off my home's deed?**

Not by opting out. The county keeps the deed, and Los Angeles County says "Records deemed to be public cannot be masked or removed from the database, unless ordered by the Court" ([LA County Registrar-Recorder](https://www.lavote.gov/home/records/real-estate-records/faqs)). Some states shield listed people, such as judges and police in Florida ([Fla. Stat. 119.071](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0119/Sections/0119.071.html)), and some Address Confidentiality Programs reach property records ([Minn. Stat. 13.045](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/13.045)). Ask a local real estate attorney what your state allows before you buy.
