There are three kinds of mailing address that aren't your home. A private mailbox (PMB) is a box at a mail-receiving business, often called a mail drop. A virtual mailbox is a private mailbox that scans your mail so you can read it online. A PO Box is a box at a Post Office. You give that address to shops, senders and anyone who asks, and your home address stays off their records.

It does not make your home address secret. The mailbox company and USPS check it and keep it on file. A REAL ID driver's license and the home-address box on a voter form still need it, and your bank needs a residential or business street address. Each section below says what an option hides and what it never hides.

## The Short Answer

A mailbox address keeps your home off the records of people you give it to. The mailbox company and USPS still have it.

1. Pick a PMB, virtual mailbox or PO Box. A PMB or virtual mailbox needs PS Form 1583, a photo ID and proof of your home address.
2. A REAL ID and the voter form's home-address box need your home. Banks need a residential or business street address.
3. If someone is a danger to you, start with your state's Address Confidentiality Program. Know this: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788).

## If someone is a danger to you, read this first

Before you act, know this: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788). The Hotline's site is [thehotline.org](https://www.thehotline.org/). The [stalking and abuse survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit) covers accounts and devices.

For your address, your state's Address Confidentiality Program (ACP) is built for this, if your state has one, and timing matters. Washington's program says applicants should not have created any local or state records at the new address they want to keep private, because it "cannot change or delete public records that were created before the person enrolled" ([Washington Secretary of State](https://www.sos.wa.gov/statewide-programs/address-confidentiality-program-acp/application-process/acp-appropriate-me)). The [ACP section below](#when-an-address-confidentiality-program-is-the-better-route) explains how it compares with a mailbox.

Setting up a box can leave a trail. If you apply for a PO Box while moving, USPS says a Post Office employee "will call you" after it checks your details, and if you join a wait list, it will contact you "by phone or by email" ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). Form 1583 for a private mailbox asks for a phone number and an email address ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). If you think someone is monitoring your devices, Safety Net, a project of the National Network to End Domestic Violence, suggests "a different device that the person hasn't had physical or remote access to in the past, and doesn't have access to now" ([Safety Net](https://www.techsafety.org/resources-survivors/technology-safety-plan)). Plan the sign-up, the payment and the timing with your advocate.

If you rent a PO Box, filing a copy of a protective order with the postmaster limits what USPS can release about it (see Option 3 below).

## Your options at a glance

| | Private mailbox (PMB) | Virtual mailbox | USPS PO Box |
|---|---|---|---|
| What you get | A street address at a mail-receiving business, with "PMB" or "#" and your box number | A private mailbox whose staff scan your mail so you read it online | A numbered box at a Post Office |
| Paperwork | PS Form 1583 and two IDs | PS Form 1583 and two IDs | USPS application and two IDs |
| Packages from UPS, FedEx or Amazon | Ask the location | Ask the provider | No, unless your Post Office offers Street Addressing |
| Hides your home from | People and companies you give the address to | The same, and you don't have to visit | The same |
| Never hides your home from | The mailbox company and USPS | The mailbox company and USPS | USPS |

Prices vary by provider and by location. USPS says the same of PO Boxes: "Prices differ depending on your location" ([USPS PO Boxes](https://www.usps.com/manage/po-boxes.htm)).

**Which one fits.**

- You need UPS, FedEx or Amazon packages: a PMB or virtual mailbox (ask the location first), or a PO Box only where the Post Office offers Street Addressing.
- You can't get to the box often: a virtual mailbox. It scans your mail, and USPS lets you sign Form 1583 on a live video call if the provider offers it.
- You want only USPS to hold your home address: a PO Box. With a PMB or virtual mailbox, the company and USPS both hold it. USPS's rules on who it may release your address to differ for each (see "What a mailbox address never hides").

**Ask before you pay:** which carriers it accepts, what it does with scans and your data, and how long it will remail your mail after you close the box. USPS rules say a mail-receiving business must remail it for at least 6 months ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

## Option 1: a private mailbox (PMB) at a mail drop

**What it is.** USPS calls the business a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA): "a business that, in whole or in part, accepts the delivery of U.S. Mail on behalf of another person or entity as a business service." It holds your mail for pickup or remails it to you with new postage, and businesses that mainly reship mail count as CMRAs too ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**How the address looks.** The business must mark your box with "PMB" or the "#" sign. USPS's example is JOE DOE, PMB 234, 10 MAIN ST STE 11, HERNDON VA 22071-2716, or on one line 10 MAIN ST PMB 234. If the mail drop's own street address already has a suite, "#" or similar, USPS says you must use "PMB", for example 10 MAIN ST STE 11 PMB 234. You can't merge the two numbers into a suite number like "10 MAIN ST STE 11-234", and USPS may return mail that is missing the PMB or # to the sender ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**What it hides.** People and companies you give the address to see the mail drop's address. USPS may tell the public that an address belongs to a mail agent, but its rules say no other information from Form 1583, "including, but not limited to, the identities of persons on whose behalf agents receive mail, may be disclosed to the public" ([39 CFR 265.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/39/265.14)).

**The limit.** The address reads as a mailbox to anyone who checks. USPS's address-checking data (DPV, which tells whether an address is a known delivery point) has a "CMRA table", CMRA being USPS's term for a mail-receiving business ([USPS PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/address-quality/dpv)), so a company that checks addresses may be able to see that yours is a mailbox. Package handling is set by each location, so ask before you rely on it.

## Option 2: a virtual mailbox that scans your mail

**What it is.** A private mailbox where the staff open and scan your mail so you can read it online, and forward it when you ask. USPS rules treat it like any other mail-receiving business: its Form 1583 asks for your remail address "or email if correspondence is scanned for digital delivery" ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**Example.** [Anytime Mailbox](/directory/anytime-mailbox), in our directory, lists "Real Street Addresses", "Mail and Package Forwarding" and "Open and Scan Mail (PDFs)" among its features. It says you'll need "two forms of ID: one with your photo and one showing your current address" and USPS Form 1583 ([Anytime Mailbox](https://www.anytimemailbox.com/)). Check its current price on its own site.

**What it hides.** The same as a private mailbox. USPS also lets you sign Form 1583 on a live video call instead of in person, if the provider offers it (see the Form 1583 section below).

**The limit.** The company opens your mail to scan it. Anytime Mailbox's site refers to "your mail center operator" and invites mail centers to "Sign Up to be an Operator" ([Anytime Mailbox](https://www.anytimemailbox.com/)), so the business at your address may be a different company from the one that runs the app. Both may handle your scans, home address and IDs, so read the privacy terms of both before you sign up. Like any mail drop, the business at your address holds your home address and your IDs.

## Option 3: a USPS PO Box

**What it is.** A box at a Post Office. You apply online or in person. USPS says "An application process for PO Box service is not completely approved until your identity and current permanent physical address where you reside or conduct business is verified" ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). You need two current IDs: a photo ID, and a second ID that verifies your address, such as a lease, mortgage, voter or vehicle registration card, or home or vehicle insurance policy ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 608.10](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/608.htm)).

**Where and when.** You can rent a box in a city or state where you don't live, "subject to availability and verification of identity and current physical address", but you pick up the keys at the Post Office where the box is. If you are moving, USPS says you can apply before the move: start verification at a Post Office where you live now, then "After you move, you will need to complete the verification process at your new address" ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). Rental terms are 3, 6 or 12 months ([USPS PO Boxes](https://www.usps.com/manage/po-boxes.htm)).

**Packages.** "Private shipping companies or carriers, such as UPS, FedEx and Amazon, are not able to deliver to (place mail in) a PO Box" ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). Some Post Offices offer Street Addressing, which lets you use the Post Office's street address with your box number and receive packages from UPS, FedEx, DHL and Amazon. USPS says this is "subject to availability and restrictions" and not offered at every location ([USPS PO Boxes](https://www.usps.com/manage/po-boxes.htm)). The address then looks like "John Smith, 123 Main Street #4567" ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.4.5.4](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**The limit.** USPS holds your home address. USPS announced in 2012 that street-addressed PO Boxes would be added to the same "CMRA table" in its address-checking data ([USPS PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/address-quality/dpv)). USPS rules also let it give a boxholder's name and address to a government agency that certifies in writing it needs them for its duties, and under a subpoena or court order. They also let it give them to a process server, a party's lawyer, or a party with no lawyer, who certifies in writing that they are needed to serve legal papers in actual or prospective litigation and gives the case details. If you file a copy of a protective order with the postmaster, the rules say information from your box application "will not be disclosed except pursuant to the order of a court of competent jurisdiction" ([39 CFR 265.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/39/265.14)). Another part of the same rule still lists exceptions for the address of someone who has filed a protective order: a government agency, a subpoena or court order, and law enforcement ([39 CFR 265.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/39/265.14)). How far this protection goes is a question for a lawyer. If someone is a danger to you, know this before you file anything: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788).

## PS Form 1583 in plain words

Form 1583 is the USPS form that lets a private or virtual mailbox accept mail for you. The item numbers below are from the June 2024 edition ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).

**Who fills it in.** For a personal box, one form for each adult using it, and spouses each need their own. A parent or guardian can list a minor without the minor's ID ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf); [USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**Two IDs.** One must be a government-issued photo ID. The second must confirm the address you list on the form, and the address must match it. Photo IDs the form lists include a state, territory or tribal driver's or nondriver's ID, a passport, a uniformed service ID, a U.S. Access Card, a permanent resident card, a certificate of naturalization, a Matricula Consular, a U.S. university ID and a NEXUS card. Address IDs it lists are a driver's or nondriver's ID, a current lease, a home or vehicle insurance policy, a mortgage or deed of trust, a vehicle registration card or a voter card. A driver's license can count as one of the two, not both ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). IDs must be current, not expired, and if the application doesn't match the ID, the business must deny it ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**Your contact details.** The form asks for a phone number and an email address (items 4d and 4e). If you plan to use an [email alias](/guides/email-aliases-simplelogin-vs-addy) or a [second phone number](/guides/get-a-second-phone-number), set them up before you apply ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).

**Who watches you sign.** You sign, or confirm your signature, in front of the mailbox company's staff or a US notary, in person or "in real-time audio and video" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). USPS allows video. Check that your mailbox company or notary offers it.

**The court-order box.** Item 4k asks "Is applicant a court-ordered protected individual?" If you answer yes, "you must attach a copy of the court order." USPS says information about someone who has filed an appropriate protective court order with the application "will not be disclosed except pursuant to the order of a court of competent jurisdiction and subject to the approval of the USPS General Counsel" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). In plain words, the form says USPS won't release it unless a court orders it and USPS's own lawyers approve. How far that holds against the other uses the form lists is a question for a lawyer.

**Where it goes.** The business uploads the form and "a clear and legible copy of each identification document" to the USPS CMRA Customer Registration Database. It keeps a copy on site for postal inspectors. Every quarter, it certifies to USPS that each form is current and no ID has expired ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)). USPS may check that you live or do business at the home address you gave ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).

**What you sign.** The signature line reads: "anyone who furnishes false or misleading information on this form or omits information requested on this form may be subject to criminal and/or civil penalties, including fines and imprisonment" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). Use your real name and real home address.

**When something changes.** "When any information required on this form changes or becomes obsolete, the applicant must file an updated application with the agent" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). After a move, that means your new home address and an address ID that shows it.

**When you close the box.** You can't file a USPS change of address from a mail drop ([USPS change of address FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). The business must remail your mail for at least 6 months, unless you tell it in writing not to, and keeps your form for at least 6 months after you leave. The remail address you give goes into the USPS database, and the business must give USPS the addresses it remails to when asked ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

## What a mailbox address never hides

- **Your home address from the mailbox company.** It checks your IDs against your home address and keeps your form ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).
- **Your home address from USPS.** The form and copies of your IDs go into its database ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)). USPS says it doesn't share that information without your consent except in listed cases, including "for law enforcement purposes", to agents or contractors, and to a USPS auditor ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)). Its rules also allow release under a subpoena or court order ([39 CFR 265.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/39/265.14)). How these lists fit together, and whether a PO Box or a PMB is better protected, is a question for a lawyer, so we don't rank them.
- **That the address is a mailbox.** See the address-checking data in Option 1.
- **Records that ask where you live.** See the next section.
- **Anything already public.** A new mailbox doesn't remove your home address from records or people-search sites that already have it. The [broker removal guide](/guides/remove-name-from-data-brokers) covers that.

## Where a mailbox address won't work

**Your bank.** Before opening an account, a US bank must get "a residential or business street address" from an individual, with fallbacks only for people who have no street address ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)). For most people that is their home address. Don't assume a bank will accept a mailbox address.

**A REAL ID driver's license or ID card.** You must show "at least two documents of the State's choice that include the individual's name and principal residence", and "A street address is required" except in listed cases ([6 CFR 37.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/6/37.11)). The card shows your principal residence, but it may show another address where state law or DMV procedure allows it, or for people enrolled in a state Address Confidentiality Program ([6 CFR 37.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/6/37.17)). Rules for licenses that aren't REAL ID vary by state, so ask your DMV.

**Voter registration.** On the federal mail-in form, Box 2 is your home address: "Do not use a post office box or rural route without a box number." Box 3 is a separate mailing address. You fill it in "If you get your mail at an address that is different from the address in Box 2" ([EAC National Mail Voter Registration Form](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/eac_assets/1/6/Federal_Voter_Registration_ENG.pdf)). What your state shows on its voter file varies by state, and we haven't checked each one, so ask your state election office.

**Landlords, lenders and utilities.** Ask what each requires and never give false details. The [moving guide](/guides/move-without-landing-on-people-search-sites) covers utilities and leases.

## The legal line

Privacy aliases are fine: a mailbox address, an email alias, a second phone number. False names or false addresses on government, bank, lease or credit paperwork are not, and this guide doesn't help with them.

- Federal law makes it a crime to knowingly and willfully make a materially false statement in a matter within the jurisdiction of the federal executive, legislative or judicial branch ([18 U.S.C. 1001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1001)).
- US banks must get a residential or business street address from an individual before opening an account, with fallbacks for people who have no street address ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)).

## When an Address Confidentiality Program is the better route

Before you act, know this: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788). See the [stalking and abuse survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit).

**What an ACP is.** A state program, also called Safe at Home in some states, for people whose safety is at risk, for example from domestic violence, stalking, harassment or rape. The National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS) says participants get "a free, forwardable address or a P.O. box to use in place of their actual address", and "each state's program will differ" ([NASS, October 2022](https://www.nass.org/sites/default/files/reports/NASS-White-Paper-Voters-ACP-Programs-Oct22.pdf)). The [moving guide's ACP step](/guides/move-without-landing-on-people-search-sites#step-5-voter-registration-drivers-license-and-state-address-confidentiality-programs) covers how people enroll and where the address can be used.

**Why it beats a mailbox for someone at risk.** In the two states we checked, state and local government agencies must accept the ACP address, with few exceptions in Washington. Washington says federal agencies don't have to (see the limits below). Washington says that, "with very few exceptions", state and local government agencies "must accept the ACP substitute address when presented a valid ACP authorization card", and the office releases a participant's real address only "in response to a valid court order or law enforcement agency request" ([Washington Secretary of State](https://www.sos.wa.gov/statewide-programs/address-confidentiality-program-acp)). California: "All California state, county and city government agencies and California courts must accept the Safe at Home designated mailing address as the participant's actual residence address" ([California Secretary of State](https://www.sos.ca.gov/registries/safe-home/frequently-asked-questions)). We found no rule that makes agencies accept a mailbox address the way these two states must accept an ACP address. Form 1583 also puts your home address with the mailbox company and USPS. A REAL ID may show the ACP address instead of your home ([6 CFR 37.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/6/37.17)).

**Timing.** Washington's program says to apply before you create state or local records at the new address (see the top of this page). Check your own state's program for its rule. Safety Net, a project of the National Network to End Domestic Violence, says a private mailbox or an ACP "is most helpful if you have recently moved or the abusive person doesn't already know your address" ([Safety Net](https://www.techsafety.org/resources-survivors/technology-safety-plan)). Any USPS change of address mails a letter to the address you are leaving ([USPS change of address FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)), so plan that with your advocate. The [moving guide](/guides/move-without-landing-on-people-search-sites) has the rest of the move.

**The limits.** An ACP is "not a crisis center or direct service agency", and "ACP participation does not guarantee safety". Private businesses and federal agencies "are not required to accept the ACP substitute address but may work with a participant upon request", and it "cannot forward packages, magazines, or marketing mail" ([Washington Secretary of State](https://www.sos.wa.gov/statewide-programs/address-confidentiality-program-acp)). California's program "does not hide participants or help them to change their identities", and it can't delete information already in public records ([California Secretary of State](https://www.sos.ca.gov/registries/safe-home/frequently-asked-questions)). If you also want a mailbox for packages, ask your ACP and your advocate first, because Form 1583 asks for your home address and an ID that shows it. Look up your own state's program, since rules differ.

## How you know it's done

- You have the full address in the right format, with "PMB" or "#" and your box number, or your PO Box number.
- The company has your approved Form 1583 on file, or USPS has verified your PO Box.
- A test letter reaches you, and for a virtual mailbox it shows up as a scan.
- A test package from the carriers you use arrives, or you know it can't.
- If someone is a danger to you: you have talked to an advocate or the Hotline, and you have applied to your state's ACP before creating new state records, or you know your state doesn't have one.
- Your senders have the mailbox address. If you're not using an ACP, your license and Box 2 of your voter registration still have your home address, and your bank has a residential or business street address.
- You have a reminder to update Form 1583 when you move or an ID expires.

## FAQ

**What is a PMB address?**

PMB stands for private mailbox, the box number you rent at a mail-receiving business. USPS requires it to be written as "PMB" or "#" with the number, for example 10 MAIN ST PMB 234. If the mail drop's street address already has a suite or "#", you must use "PMB", for example 10 MAIN ST STE 11 PMB 234 ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**What ID do I need for a virtual mailbox or mail drop?**

Two current IDs on PS Form 1583: a government-issued photo ID, and a second that shows the home address on the form, such as a lease, insurance policy, vehicle registration or voter card. A driver's license counts for only one of the two ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf); [USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).

**Can I sign Form 1583 online?**

Yes, if your mailbox company or a notary offers a live video call. USPS lets you sign or confirm your signature in front of the mailbox company's staff or a US notary, in person or "in real-time audio and video" ([PS Form 1583](https://about.usps.com/forms/ps1583.pdf)).

**Can UPS and FedEx deliver to a PO Box?**

Not to a plain PO Box. USPS says private carriers "are not able to deliver to (place mail in) a PO Box" ([USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). Some Post Offices offer Street Addressing, which lets you receive packages from UPS, FedEx, DHL and Amazon at the Post Office's street address ([USPS PO Boxes](https://www.usps.com/manage/po-boxes.htm)).

**Is a virtual mailbox more private than a PO Box?**

Neither hides your home from its operator. A mailbox company checks and stores your home address and IDs and uploads them to USPS, and USPS verifies a PO Box holder's home or business address too ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm); [USPS PO Box FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/PO-Box-The-Basics)). USPS has different release rules for each ([39 CFR 265.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/39/265.14)), and which is better protected is a question for a lawyer.

**Can I open a bank account with a mailbox address?**

Don't count on it. US banks must get "a residential or business street address" from an individual before opening an account ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)). For most people that is their home address.

**Can I put a mailbox address on my driver's license or voter registration?**

Not as your home address. A REAL ID needs documents showing your principal residence and a street address ([6 CFR 37.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/6/37.11)), and the federal voter form says not to use a post office box for your home address, though it has a separate box for a mailing address ([EAC](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/eac_assets/1/6/Federal_Voter_Registration_ENG.pdf)). Other rules vary by state. If you are at risk, a state Address Confidentiality Program address may be usable on both ([NASS](https://www.nass.org/sites/default/files/reports/NASS-White-Paper-Voters-ACP-Programs-Oct22.pdf)). Know this: changing a setting or signing a device out can alert the person watching, so plan visible changes with a trained advocate; in the US the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential and open 24/7 (call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788). See the [stalking and abuse survivor safety kit](/stacks/stalking-abuse-survivor-safety-kit).

**Can people tell my address is a mail drop?**

Often, yes. The address must show "PMB" or "#" ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)), USPS may confirm that an address belongs to a mail agent ([39 CFR 265.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/39/265.14)), and USPS address data has a "CMRA table", CMRA being USPS's term for a mail-receiving business ([USPS PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/address-quality/dpv)).

**What happens to my mail when I close a private mailbox?**

You can't file a USPS change of address from it ([USPS change of address FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/Knowledge/Change-of-Address-The-Basics)). The business must remail your mail for at least 6 months unless you tell it in writing not to, and the remail address goes into the USPS database ([USPS Domestic Mail Manual 508.1.8](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/508.htm)).
