EPIC is the privacy world's watchdog in Washington D.C. — using FOIA requests, lawsuits, and Congressional testimony to hold governments and corporations accountable for privacy violations.

## What They Do

### Freedom of Information (FOIA) Litigation
- EPIC files more **FOIA lawsuits** against federal agencies than almost any other organization
- Forced disclosure of NSA surveillance documents, TSA body scanner risks, and facial recognition deployments
- Made government surveillance programs public that agencies wanted to keep secret

### Legal Advocacy
- Files **amicus curiae** (friend of the court) briefs in major privacy cases
- Challenged the NSA's phone metadata collection program
- Opposed Clearview AI's facial recognition database
- Advocated before the Supreme Court in landmark privacy cases

### Congressional Testimony
- Provides expert testimony on privacy legislation
- Testified on GDPR-style federal privacy law, AI regulation, and surveillance reform
- One of the most-cited privacy organizations in Congressional hearings

### Research & Public Education
- Publishes annual reports on privacy developments
- Maintains resources on consumer privacy, AI ethics, and government surveillance
- Tracks state and federal privacy legislation

## Key Victories

- Played a role in establishing the **Privacy Act of 1974**
- Contributed to **COPPA** (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) development
- Challenged the TSA's body scanner program
- FOIA'd documents revealing FBI's use of facial recognition
- Advocated for the EU-US **Privacy Shield** replacement after Schrems II

## Why EPIC Matters

While the EFF focuses broadly on digital rights and technology policy, EPIC specifically targets the **intersection of privacy and government power** — making it essential for accountability when the government's own surveillance programs operate in secrecy.
