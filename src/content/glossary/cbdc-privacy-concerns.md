Central Bank Digital Currencies represent a fundamental shift from cash-based financial privacy to programmable, surveilled money.

## What CBDCs Are

- Digital currency issued directly by central banks
- Replaces or supplements physical cash
- Every transaction is recorded on a central ledger
- Programmable — can include restrictions on how money is spent

## Privacy Risks

- **Total financial surveillance**: Every transaction visible to the government
- **Programmable restrictions**: Money that can't be spent on certain goods or at certain stores
- **Expiration dates**: Money that must be spent by a certain date
- **Social credit integration**: Spending privileges tied to behavior scores
- **Instant freezing**: Any account can be frozen without court order

## Cash vs CBDC

- Cash transactions are private by default
- CBDC transactions are surveilled by default
- The elimination of cash removes the last truly private payment method

## Defense

1. Advocate for preserving physical cash
2. Use privacy cryptocurrencies for transactions requiring financial privacy
3. Support legislation requiring privacy protections in CBDC design
4. Diversify into assets outside the digital banking system
