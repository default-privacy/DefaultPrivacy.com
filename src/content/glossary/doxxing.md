Doxxing is one of the most personal and damaging privacy violations. It turns online conflicts into real-world danger.

## How Doxxers Find Information

- **Data broker sites**: Spokeo, WhitePages, BeenVerified sell personal details for a few dollars
- **Public records**: Property records, voter registration, court filings
- **Social media**: Photos with metadata, check-ins, friend connections reveal locations
- **WHOIS records**: Domain registrations expose name, address, phone
- **LLC filings**: Business registrations in most states are public record
- **Data breaches**: Leaked databases contain names, addresses, passwords

## Who Gets Doxxed

- Content creators and streamers
- Journalists and activists
- Anyone involved in online controversy
- Business owners (via public filings)
- Dating app users (via location data)

## How to Protect Yourself

1. **Remove yourself from data brokers** — Use a removal service or opt out manually from each site
2. **Keep the house off public filings you control** — a mail receiving address, and privacy protection on any domain you register
3. **Use privacy-focused domain registration** — Register domains through a privacy registrar
4. **Lock down social media** — Audit what's publicly visible, remove location data
5. **Use separate identities** for online activity — different email, username, and persona per platform
6. **Monitor your exposure** — Regularly search for your own information online

## If You've Been Doxxed

1. Document everything (screenshots with timestamps)
2. Report to platforms where the information was posted
3. Contact law enforcement if threats are involved
4. Engage a digital erasure service for comprehensive removal
5. Consider an emergency name change in extreme cases
